# Montreal: how crowds arrive at the region's large venues

Research for Fan/Friction. Answer date: October 7, 2026.

**Labels.** *Official* = published by an operator, team, venue or government body. *Reported* = a figure attributed to an official source but found only in news coverage. *Estimated* = my own number, built from the anchors cited next to it. Every row says which.

**The short version.** Montreal is an **in-between region** whose core behaves like a transit region. No large venue in the region reaches 80% by car. The downtown buildings and the island festivals sit well under half by car; Laval and the amusement-park side of Parc Jean-Drapeau are majority car. The only hard, observed counts are the STM's station figures for the Bell Centre and the Grand Prix's 97% transit/active share. Almost everything else is an estimate anchored on parking supply and the ARTM survey, and is labelled that way.

## 2. Venue detail

### 2.1 Bell Centre

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| Car share | **35% (range 30–45), estimated.** What remains after the observed Métro floor and the transit/walk shares below. |
| Transit share | **50% (45–55), estimated, with an observed floor of ~24%.** The STM said game days in the regular season add about 5,000 riders at Lucien-L'Allier and Bonaventure; on May 12, 2026 (playoffs, Buffalo Game 4) it was "8000 usagers de plus qu'un mardi normal" — *8,000 more riders than a normal Tuesday* — at those two stations ([La Presse, May 15, 2026](https://www.lapresse.ca/actualites/grand-montreal/2026-05-15/le-canadien-en-series/des-records-d-achalandage-pour-le-rem.php)). 5,000 ÷ 21,105 = 24%, and that counts only two stations. It misses fans using Peel, Guy-Concordia, Georges-Vanier or Square-Victoria; the REM at Gare Centrale; exo trains at Gare Lucien-L'Allier and Gare Centrale; and buses. The REM's daily ridership jumped from ~78,000 to ~104,000 on that playoff day, a record the REM credited partly to the Canadiens ([Castanet/CP](https://www.castanetkamloops.net/news/Canada/614742/Canadiens-playoff-run-has-increased-ridership-on-Montreal-s-light-rail-network)). |
| Stations, walk | Lucien-L'Allier (Orange), connected, 2–3 min. Bonaventure (Orange), via the underground network, ~5 min. REM Gare Centrale (South Shore, Deux-Montagnes and Anse-à-l'Orme branches), ~7–10 min. exo commuter rail at Gare Lucien-L'Allier, next door. The STM itself points fans to Bonaventure and Lucien-L'Allier ([STM event page](https://www.stm.info/en/node/598)). |
| Walk, bike, other | **15%, estimated.** Downtown residents walk; the downtown population grew 23% between 2018 and 2023 to about 111,000 ([ARTM 2023 highlights](https://www.artm.quebec/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ARTM_Enquete2023_Faits_Saillants.pdf)). |
| Basis | STM event-day station increments (observed, reported via La Presse); ARTM 2023 downtown commute shares (bound). The building opened in 1996 with a new commuter-rail station built into the project ([ArcelorMittal case study](https://long-canada.arcelormittal.com/en/case-study/bell-centre/)). I found **no** Canadiens or evenko published transit share for games or concerts. |
| Parking | On-site underground garage, flat event rate; **space count not found**. The arena itself says many garages surround it and are unaffiliated ([Ticketmaster venue page](https://www.ticketmaster.ca/centre-bell-tickets-montreal/venue/401966?page=3)). Example: Le 1000 de la Gauchetière, 550+ spaces, with a Bell Centre event rate ([Le 1000](https://le1000.com/en/access-and-services/parking/)). Most drivers use private garages. |
| Event-day setup | STM says the Métro runs more often on game nights ([STM](https://www.stm.info/en/node/598)); Orange Line every 4 minutes on playoff nights ([Journal Métro](https://journalmetro.com/actualites/montreal/3215541/grand-prix-et-hockey-difficile-de-se-deplacer-a-montreal/)). Avenue des Canadiens-de-Montréal is a pedestrian street. |
| Winter | Indoor. **No published evidence** that snow days shift the share toward the Métro. The underground connection to both stations makes it plausible; unmeasured. |

## 3. The region

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Driving, transit, or in between? | **In between, with a transit-region core.** Region-wide, 66% of all weekday trips are by car (2023) ([ARTM](https://www.artm.quebec/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ARTM_Enquete2023_Faits_Saillants.pdf)). But big crowds concentrate where the Métro is: the Bell Centre, the downtown festival sites and Parc Jean-Drapeau all sit at or below about a third by car, and the Grand Prix is effectively car-free. Off the core, Place Bell and La Ronde are majority car, but **no venue reaches the 80% driving-region line**. |
| Closest analogue | Closer to the New York region than to Seattle for downtown nights; Laval behaves like a mid-sized suburban arena. |

## 5. Playoff watch parties outside the Bell Centre

| Question | Finding |
|---|---|
| Published size estimate | **None found** for May 25, 2026 (East Final Game 3 vs Carolina) or any 2026 night. Coverage says only "thousands". The club's own figures are not a head count: three giant screens, site open from 5:00 p.m., and 2,500 rally towels handed out per event ([Canadiens, May 25, 2026](https://www.nhl.com/canadiens/news/watch-party-street-edition-returns-for-ecf-may-25-2026)). For scale, a 2021 Stanley Cup Final report quoted a guess of ~25,000 outside when COVID rules held the arena to 3,500 ([PressReader/Mercury News](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-mercury-news/20210703/281981790574361)) — a different era and not comparable. |
| How they arrive | **Not published.** The best clue: the STM's game-day increment at Lucien-L'Allier + Bonaventure was ~5,000 in the regular season but ~8,000 on a playoff Tuesday ([La Presse](https://www.lapresse.ca/actualites/grand-montreal/2026-05-15/le-canadien-en-series/des-records-d-achalandage-pour-le-rem.php)). The ~3,000 difference is plausibly mostly the street crowd (estimated; playoff ticket-holders may also ride more). Treat the watch party as a **transit-and-walk crowd** — it is unticketed, downtown, and draws heavily from people already in the core. |
| Context on May 25 | Monday night, 8:00 p.m. puck drop, the day after the Grand Prix weekend (May 22–24). |
