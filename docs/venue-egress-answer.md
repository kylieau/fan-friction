## Why the lane column is the hard part

Every venue's lane count is uncertain because none is published. Imagery would be the only source, and it can't be counted reliably from here. Even a careful count from imagery misses three things, and about half the list falls into one of these groups.

1. **Stack-parked venues.** At the Rose Bowl, the Hollywood Bowl and likely Empire Polo, cars leave as fast as the stacks unpack, not as fast as the gate lanes allow. Lanes overstate throughput there.

2. **Event-night setups that imagery doesn't show.** Ohio Stadium and the Rose Bowl convert streets to one-way outbound. Empire Polo sets temporary gates for each festival. Citi Field's lots are changing under construction. FivePoint would need historical imagery, and even that wouldn't show which gates were open on show nights.

3. **Downtown and shared-district venues with no gate to count.** Crypto.com, Peacock, Golden 1, Petco, T-Mobile, Wintrust, Amalie and the Phoenix arena have no venue lots. The Inglewood four and Expo Park share lots, so the cars ÷ lanes rule has no clear denominator. Golden 1 gives the best substitute found: a city-measured count of cars per sellout (about 7,000) instead of spaces.

The Dodgers' own claim that every gate reaches every freeway also hints that the queue forms downstream, not at the gate. So gate-lane counts may set the floor on clearance time rather than the actual time.

Since v1 has to run on public data and eventually outside LA, the rule as written depends on a hand count most venues worldwide won't have. A version you could compute everywhere would estimate cars as attendance × drive share ÷ occupancy. For outbound throughput, it would count the lanes on roads crossing a ring about 1 km around the venue, using OpenStreetMap lane tags. Those tags are uneven, but they are public and global. That's a design change.
