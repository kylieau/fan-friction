# New-city checklist

Kylie, Oct 6, 2026: Los Angeles is the only city built by hand. Every later city rides this page and the scripts. If a step can't be put here, the feature isn't done. A second city is for testing that the formula holds outside LA (step 4 of `docs/product/big-picture-plan-oct6.md`), not for coverage.

## 1. Configure (a few lines)
- `src/config/metros.ts`: id, name, time zone, map center and zoom.
- `src/data/formula/gridlock.ts` `CITY_TYPE`: sprawl (everyone drives), hub, or transit. This sets the spill between zones and the default car share for a venue without its own. Research it, don't assume it (Kylie, Oct 6): run `docs/research/city-builds/seattle/seattle-city-type-prompt.md` with the city swapped in, and give each venue its `carShare` from the answer.
- `src/data/formula/overlap.ts`: the city's **broad teams** (everyone's team, by year), if any.
- `src/data/formula/weather.ts` `MONTHLY_NORMAL_F`: the heat baseline by month (empty today; the 85°F floor applies until filled). Needs a source that works for every city.
- `src/data/scheduleArchive.ts` / `scripts/weather-fetch.mjs` / `scripts/results-fetch.mjs` / `scripts/catalog-write.mjs` (`ARCHIVE_METRO_ID`, `RESULTS_METRO_ID`): the metro ids each job covers (LA only today). The shared catalog tables are keyed by metro, so a second city needs no new tables.

## 2. Teams (one line each)
- `src/data/teams.ts`: a record per team and college program (id, names, league, sport, metro).
- `src/data/sources/mlbSource.ts` `MLB_TEAMS` and `MLB_VENUES`: MLB id and ballpark id.
- `src/data/sources/espnSource.ts` `ESPN_TEAMS` and `VENUE_BY_NAME`: ESPN path and id per team; the venue names ESPN uses. Ids are in `docs/reference/data-sources.md`'s method: the team page on espn.com carries the id in its URL.
- `scripts/attendance-collect.mjs`: the same ids with the seasons to pull.
- `src/data/sources/sidearmSchools.ts`: one line per school whose athletics site runs on Sidearm Sports (the footer says so; `https://<site>/calendar.ashx/calendar.ics` returns `text/calendar`). Give its site, the name its feed writes before the sport ("USC Trojans") and a short name. Its games then arrive nightly, exhibitions included, placed by the room in the feed's LOCATION, so the school's rooms of 1,000+ must be in `venues.ts` under the names the feed uses. A school on WMT Digital or another provider is left out; its home games come through opponents' feeds. **Probe (Oct 22, 2026):** `curl -sI https://<site>/calendar.ashx/calendar.ics | grep -i content-type` says `text/calendar` on a Sidearm site and 404 elsewhere; read the first SUMMARY lines for the prefix. Where the feed spells a room differently from `venues.ts` ("UWM Panther Arena", "Michael J. Hagan '85 Arena"), add the spelling to that room's `aliases` (matched, never shown), not to `names` (a rename). Where the prefix carries the side ("University of Alberta Pandas"), set `sides`. A game links to a team page when the school word and the sport words of a program in `teams.ts` match the feed's (`schoolWord`, `sportWords`), so a program people would follow wants a record. **Rooms the feed names that are not on file (Oct 22, 2026):** read the feed's home rows, list the rooms that place nowhere, and for each of about 1,000 seats and up add a venue record with a figure read on the school's facility page (or Wikipedia, labeled reported), an OpenStreetMap point (or the campus or address point, said so in the comment) and the feed's spelling in `aliases`; a room with no published figure stays in BACKLOG, not in the table. Setups the capacity type does not know (volleyball, softball, field hockey) go in the note, not the field. A school-feed game in any sport but football, basketball, hockey and baseball sits under the floor whatever the room (`SCHOOL_FEED_ARENA_SPORTS` in read.ts), so a 22,000-seat track bowl used for soccer does not read as a friction crowd.

## 3. Venues (the hand part, about an afternoon)
- Run the venue research prompt for the city (`docs/research/city-builds/los-angeles/la-venue-table-prompt.md`, change the city and boundary). Fold the answer into `src/data/venues.ts`: names with rename dates, coordinates, capacity by setup and year, roof. **Names vs. aliases (Kylie, Oct 22, 2026):** a name the room has really gone by goes in `names`, with its start date (`from`) when a source gives one; a name with no date never shows on any date, so a former name without one leaves old nights under a later name. A seller's, a feed's or a short spelling goes in `aliases` (matched by every lookup, never shown). Label every figure official, reported or estimated. Geocode coordinates against OpenStreetMap.
- Save the answer under `docs/` and point `docs/reference/data-sources.md` at it. If it arrived as a PDF, save the PDF too and transcribe it with its links (`node scripts/pdf-text.mjs file.pdf`); a paste in the chat is not a saved source.
- **Ticketmaster venue IDs (decided Oct 12, C12).** Look up and verify a Ticketmaster venue ID for every concert room (`node scripts/ticketmaster-venues.mjs --metro=<id>`, or the research answer's ID checked against the Ticketmaster venue page). A room without one gets no concerts, so a city with none ships with no concerts. List any room you couldn't verify in the city's BACKLOG follow-up. The cities built without IDs get one catch-up lookup pass at the end of the current build round.
- **Research prompts.** A new city's city-type and venue-table prompts must include the "Gaps from the first 26 city builds" section, as the templates do (`docs/research/city-builds/los-angeles/la-venue-table-prompt.md`, `docs/research/city-builds/seattle/seattle-city-type-prompt.md`, `docs/research/city-builds/seattle/seattle-venue-table-prompt.md`). Don't run a prompt that leaves it out.

## 4. Run the scripts
1. `npm run attendance-collect` then `npm run attendance-calibrate`: past crowds and expected draws per team.
2. `node scripts/venue-access.mjs`: relief and streets per venue; the hard-access flag follows from the rule.
3. Run `node scripts/weather-fetch.mjs --metro=<id>`, `node --env-file=.env.local scripts/archive-schedule.mjs --metro=<id>` and `node scripts/results-fetch.mjs --metro=<id>` to confirm the feeds, weather and results for the new metro. The optional `--metro` also works on `catalog-write.mjs`, `ticketmaster-venues.mjs` and `venue-access.mjs`; without it the existing all-city job is unchanged. After pushing, run `gh workflow run schedule-archive.yml --ref main -f metro=<id>` so the shared catalog can be published and checked independently of other cities. Scheduled runs still cover every city. The nightly job also writes each team's full schedule (`team_schedules`); a team is covered there as soon as it is in `ESPN_TEAMS` or `MLB_TEAMS`.
4. `node scripts/retune-analysis.mjs` only when the city has a season of archived nights.

## 5. Check on screen
San Diego (Oct 6, 2026) was the first run of this list, about three hours end to end: steps 1, 2 and 4 in an hour (the Padres, the city facts, the jobs' city list, the attendance pull); step 3 from Kylie's one research run (`docs/research/city-builds/san-diego/san-diego-venue-table-answer.md`, 13 venues folded in, including Frontwave Arena, which the brief had missed); then SDSU, San Diego FC and the Wave joined the feed and the attendance pull.

Seattle (Oct 6, 2026) was the second run, about ninety minutes once the research answer was in: eight teams and the city facts in one commit, 21 venues folded in (`docs/research/city-builds/seattle/seattle-venue-table-answer.md`), the attendance pull and calibration (12 minutes), the hard-access measure (about 25 minutes, Open-Elevation's pace), one hand run of `archive-schedule`, then the nightly job by hand so the catalog had the city the same day. Two things learned: the venue table's `setup` has no festival kind (store festival caps as `concert` with a note), and ESPN's short name for a soccer club can be the city ("Seattle"), so game titles now take our own short name for a home team we know.

New York (Oct 7, 2026) was the third run, about two and a half hours once both research answers were in, committed and pushed after every step: steps 1–2 in forty minutes (18 teams; **three of the ids written down in advance were wrong** — nhl/1 is the Bruins, nwsl/15362 is Portland, cfb/2523 is Robert Morris — so every id was checked against the feed first, and ESPN's venue names were read from each team's own schedule rather than guessed); step 3 in an hour (33 venues from `docs/research/city-builds/new-york/new-york-venue-table-answer.md`, a car share on each from `docs/research/city-builds/new-york/new-york-city-type-answer.md`, every coordinate checked against OpenStreetMap by name, five moved); step 4 in forty minutes (attendance pull and calibration, one hand archive run, the hard-access measure at Open-Elevation's pace). Four things learned: **research the city type before touching `CITY_TYPE`** — the code had New York as transit from before any research, and the measured answer is hub; a plain team id may already belong to an away team (`giants`, `jets`, `rangers` were San Francisco, Winnipeg and Texas), so prefix it; the research's "Table B" grounds with no fixed capacity (the US Open grounds, Javits, festival parks) are not venue rows — events there are placed as points and sized by their own crowd; and the feeds serve today onward, so a tester's nights from *before* the city was covered need reconstructed reads from saved research, a separate step.

Atlanta (Oct 7, 2026) was the fourth run, about ninety minutes to a saved listing once both research answers were in, committed and pushed after every step. Steps 1–2 in half an hour (14 teams; every id checked against the feed first, all correct this time; the Braves already existed as a visitor record and became the home record, same id); step 3 in forty minutes (20 venues, each with a car share; 16 coordinates confirmed by OpenStreetMap, 3 rooms it does not know taken from the research and marked approximate); step 4: the attendance pull (two minutes), one triggered nightly run (three minutes; Atlanta's first listing had 32 events, 27 with an estimate), and the hard-access measure after an Open-Elevation rate limit (wait five minutes and retry). Five things learned: **a research answer may correct the brief itself** (six venue names had changed since the prompt was written: EchoPark Speedway, Gwinnett Field, Synovus Bank Amphitheater, Lakewood's dropped sponsor), so read the answer's own "out of date" notes before writing rows; **ESPN names some buildings by an interior brand** (the KSU Convocation Center is "VyStar Arena", Fifth Third Stadium is "Walens Family Field at Fifth Third Stadium"), so take the names from each team's own schedule as before; **neutral-site games are hand-listed** (`listings2026.ts`) because neither side is a home team in the feeds; **festival parks and convention halls are not venue rows** (Kylie, Oct 7), as New York's Table B grounds were not; and a city's Explore screen says "No events" until the nightly job has written its catalog, so trigger the job right after step 3 and check the screen after it lands.

The Bay Area (Oct 7, 2026) was the fifth run, about seventy minutes to a saved listing once both research answers were in, committed and pushed after every step. Steps 1–2 in twenty minutes (17 teams, including the Oakland Roots for their last Coliseum match; every id checked against the feed first, and one guess was wrong: Bay FC is nwsl/22187, not 22530; the Giants already existed as a visitor record and became the home record, same id, as the Braves had); step 3 in twenty-five minutes (20 venues, each with a car share from the city-type answer; 18 coordinates confirmed by OpenStreetMap within about 100 m, Frost and the SJSU event center taken from the research and marked approximate); step 4: the attendance pull (one team at a time: `node scripts/attendance-collect.mjs <teamId>` takes a team id, not a city), calibration, one hand archive run, the triggered nightly run (27 events, 13 of them Ticketmaster), and the hard-access measure (about fifteen minutes, no rate limit this time). Four things learned: **the hard-access rule and the research disagree in both directions** (the rule flags Cow Palace, the Concord pavilion and Haas; the research calls Shoreline single-approach and Memorial Stadium and the Greek constrained, none of which the rule flags), so a city's flags are recorded as the rule's and the research's judgment stays in the venue notes; **a team's short name must match ESPN's display name or its games take ESPN's city name** ("Oakland vs. San Antonio" until the Roots' record said "Oakland Roots"); **a team can announce more than its building's listed size** (the 49ers announce about 71,500 at Levi's, listed at 68,500), which the estimate shows honestly beside Seats; and a metro with three centers 30–45 miles apart (San Francisco, Oakland, San Jose) reads every night as one crowd, which is the case for the distance discount (`docs/archive/proposals/distance-discount-proposal.md`).

Chicago (Oct 7, 2026) was the sixth run (its 23 `data/venue-access.tsv` rows were saved Oct 8, not Oct 7, by the full re-run; none is flagged), about forty-five minutes to a saved listing once both research answers were in. Steps 1–2 in fifteen minutes (15 teams; every id checked against the feed first, and one guess was wrong again: DePaul is 305, not 2305; Loyola left out because Gentile Arena is under 5,000; the metro already existed as an away market for Kylie's log, so its frame was widened rather than added); step 3 in fifteen minutes (23 venues, the old Wintrust row replaced in place; 20 coordinates confirmed by OpenStreetMap, the Tinley Park amphitheatre taken from the research; the research's one boundary addition, Chicagoland Speedway, kept with a note); step 4: the attendance pull, calibration, one hand archive run (13 events, the White Sox ALDS sized by the new playoff rule), the triggered nightly run, and the hard-access measure (no venue flagged: a flat city). Two things learned: **a team's past home building needs its feed name in the map too**, not only its current one (the Stars played 2022–25 at SeatGeek Stadium, and the first pull found 4 games until "seatgeek stadium" was added); and **a city type set before research is a guess to replace**, as New York's was: Chicago sat as 'transit' from the first away-market stub, and the research reads it as in between, leaning driving.

Dallas–Fort Worth (Oct 8, 2026) was the seventh run, about an hour to a saved listing. Steps 1–2 in fifteen minutes (15 teams, every id checked against the feed first, all correct; the Rangers already existed as a visitor record and became the home record); step 3 in twenty-five minutes (30 venues, each with a car share; 26 coordinates confirmed by OpenStreetMap, which moved the Grand Prairie pair about two miles from the research's guesses, as the research warned; the Tinley-style ones it does not know taken from the research); the State Fair hand-listed as a daily point event for its remaining eleven days using the official 2025 gate for the same day of the run, and the Red River Showdown as a neutral-site game inside it (the fair's Saturday figure less the stadium crowd, so the two are not counted twice); step 4: the attendance pull, calibration, the hard-access measure (no venue flagged), and one hand archive run that **failed twice on "fetch failed" before it ran**: the machine's DNS had dropped, not ESPN. Check `curl` against a known site before blaming a feed. One thing learned: **a team can announce more than its building's seats** again (the Cowboys announce 92,000+ against 80,000 seated, with standing-room platforms), so the estimate sits above Seats as it does for the 49ers.

Montreal (Oct 8, 2026) was the eighth run and the first outside the United States, about fifty minutes to a saved listing. Steps 1–2 in fifteen minutes (two teams on the feeds, the Canadiens and CF Montréal, ids checked; four more records with no feed yet, the Alouettes, Victoire, Rocket and Carabins, so their pages say "No schedule yet" until a source is wired: `docs/product/no-feed-teams-proposal.md`); step 3 in twenty minutes (8 venues, every coordinate confirmed by OpenStreetMap; the research's one headline correction, the Bell Centre at 20,962 from 2025–26, carried as a dated capacity row); the tester's Canadiens night (May 25, 2026) hand-seeded in `past2026.ts` from `docs/research/events-and-dates/past-dates/san-diego-montreal.md`, with the street watch party left out because no count was published; step 4: the attendance pull, calibration, one hand archive run (6 events), the triggered nightly run, and the hard-access measure after an Open-Elevation rate limit (wait five minutes). Three things learned: **a Canadian city needs nothing special** (time zone `America/Toronto`, Open-Meteo, Open-Elevation, Nominatim and Ticketmaster all answer; the feeds carry the Canadiens and CF Montréal as any other club); **a venue's name in the research is often the French one and ESPN's the English one**, so both go in the venue's names and in the feed map; and **a city whose big nights are open sites** (the Grand Prix, Osheaga, the Jazz Festival) has a thinner building list than its crowds suggest, so the hand-listed point events matter more here than anywhere yet.
