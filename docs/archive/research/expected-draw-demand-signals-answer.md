# Answer: what makes one game draw more than another like it?

Answer to `docs/expected-draw-demand-signals.md` (Oct 7, 2026). Researched Oct 6, 2026 (Pacific). Outside researcher, no app code seen. For Claude Code to turn into a proposal; nothing here is approved for building.

**How to read this**
- "Test now" = values for past games (2022 onward) exist from a free, allowed source, so the hide-and-predict test can run today.
- "Record now" = no usable history; start saving today's value per game and test after a season.
- "Reject" = paid, against terms, no history and can't be recorded, or it is really the calendar again.
- Effect sizes below are only the ones the cited studies report. Nothing is invented. Anything illustrative is marked *(illustrative)*.
- "Not checked" means I did not verify it in this pass; treat it as an open item, not a fact.

---

## Three things that change how every factor is tested

1. **Sellouts hide effects.** Announced crowds stop at capacity. A factor can only show up for teams that don't sell out every night. Studies of NBA stars had to model that ceiling explicitly ([Humphreys & Johnson, WVU working paper 17-16](https://researchrepository.wvu.edu/econ_working-papers/231)). Expect many factors to pass for MLB, MLS, NWSL, WNBA and some NHL/NBA teams and do nothing for the NFL and sold-out arenas. That is a result, not a failure.
2. **Big draws move venues.** Messi and Caitlin Clark games were moved into bigger stadiums: Sporting KC hosted Inter Miami at Arrowhead and drew 72,610 ([MLS mid-2024 milestones via 90min](https://www.90min.com/posts/major-league-soccer-cites-lionel-messi-effect-in-2024-attendance-milestones)); the Fever's road games prompted a trend of flexing to bigger arenas ([The GIST](https://www.thegistsports.com/article/indiana-fever-report-wnba-highs-in-attendance-social-media-engagement-and-team-sponsors)). **The capacity cap must come from the venue on that game's schedule entry, not the home team's usual building.** This is a data fix, not a factor.
3. **Most of the strongest factors need no new source.** Standings before the game, last season's record and title, past playoff meetings, same-city pairs, how long since a visitor last came, and whether the visitor draws on the road can all be derived from the game history the app already keeps (schedules, results, announced attendance). Start there.

## 2. Short rules, as they would be tested

Every value uses only facts known **before the game's date** (the owner's rule). "Prior season" means the last completed season.

**Promotions**
- **P1** — For each MLB home game: has a bobblehead (yes/no); has any other giveaway (yes/no); has fireworks (yes/no); has a ticket discount (yes/no); has a theme night (yes/no). Test each type separately, and test "weekday × any promotion" separately, because the studies find weekday promos matter most.
- **P2** — Same yes/no flags for other leagues, saved from each team's published promo page the week before the game.

**Standings and storylines**
- **S1** — Home team in a playoff position the morning of the game: yes/no. Plus games back (MLB, NBA, WNBA) or points back (NHL, MLS, NWSL) of the last playoff spot, from results before the game. College: home team ranked in the latest AP poll before the game: yes/no *(AP source not checked; Wikipedia season pages list weekly ranks)*.
- **S2** — Game falls in the last 20% of the home schedule **and** home team is within 5 games / 6 points of a spot *(thresholds illustrative; test 3 values)*.
- **S3** — Home team's win % this season so far minus last season's final win %; and last season's final win %. Skip the first ~10% of games, when "so far" is noise.
- **S4** — Home team won last season's title: yes/no.
- **S5** — Visiting team won last season's title: yes/no.
- **S6** — Home team reached last season's final (or semifinal round): yes/no.
- **S7** — Home team won last season's title after ≥ 20 seasons without one: yes/no *(threshold illustrative)*.
- **S8** — Home team's head coach (or principal owner) is new this season: yes/no.

**Stars**
- **ST1** — Visiting roster includes a player whose Wikipedia article ranked in his/her league's top 10 by total page views over the prior season: yes/no; also the count of such players. *(Top 10 illustrative; test 5, 10, 25.)* Roster = the team's active roster **seven days before** the game.
- **ST2** — Same, for the home roster.
- **ST3** — Same rules, with "named an All-Star last season" in place of page views; also "first-team All-League last season".
- **ST4** — Same rules, with "in the latest published official jersey top 15 before the game" (NBA) or "in the latest NFLPA Top 50" (NFL).
- **ST5** — Visitor's road-draw index = average over earlier games (this season and the prior two) of (announced crowd ÷ that host's expected crowd from the calendar model), when this team was the visitor. Needs announced attendance at every building in the league, not only the five cities. *Fallback if only the five cities' home games are stored:* the same ratio, but only from this visitor's earlier games in **this** building.
- **Injury and rest** — Count a star only if not on the injured/inactive list seven days before. **Ignore day-of scratches.** Reason: tickets are mostly bought in advance; when Clark missed early 2025 games, road crowds barely moved ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/06/06/sports/wnba-caitlin-clark-indiana-fever/)). A season-ending injury announced weeks ahead does count (the player drops off the active roster). Past injured-list dates: MLB's feed likely has transactions *(not checked)*; other leagues not checked — if unavailable, test with "on the roster seven days before" only.

**Attention**
- **A1** — Median daily page views of (a) the visiting team's article, (b) the home team's article, (c) the pair's rivalry article if one exists, over the 28 days ending 7 days before the game, each divided by that article's median over the prior season. Above 1 = more attention than usual. Using a window that ends a week early keeps news about the game itself out.

**Rivalry and history**
- **R1** — Both teams' home venues are in the same metro: yes/no.
- **R2** — Game appears on the Wikipedia trophy/cup list for that season: yes/no.
- **R3** — Visitor is the home team's league-designated rival (MLB) / division rival (others): yes/no.
- **R4** — Pair's combined Know Rivalry score (0–200); test as a number and as "≥ 100: yes/no" *(cut-off illustrative)*.
- **R5** — The two teams met in the playoffs within the last 1 / 3 / 10 seasons: yes/no (three separate tests).
- **R6** — Their previous meeting had a fighting major or game misconduct: yes/no (NHL).
- **R7** — Seasons since the visitor last played a game in this building: number, and "≥ 3: yes/no" *(illustrative)*. A first-ever visit counts as the maximum.

**Other**
- **O1** — Forecast high at game time ≥ 90°F (outdoor only): yes/no; rain forecast: yes/no. In the past-games test, use observed weather and label the result an upper bound, since a week-out forecast is less accurate.
- **O2** — Seasons since the venue opened: 0, 1, 2, 3–5, 6+.
- **O3** — Another 5,000+ event in the same metro starting within 2 hours: yes/no; and specifically another major-league game: yes/no.
- **O4** — Home opener: yes/no; last regular-season home game: yes/no.
- **O5** — Number of players on either roster who played in the most recent Olympics/World Cup (NHL, NWSL).

## 4. Order: test these three first

1. **Standings and last season (S1 + S3, then S4–S6).** Strongest and most consistent evidence in the research; all leagues; no new source, since everything comes from results already in the app. Cheapest to build and the most likely to pass.
2. **Who the visitor is (ST5 road-draw index, with ST1 page-view stars as the challenger and ST3 All-Stars as the baseline).** The research on star visitors is strong (NBA, WNBA, MLS), and ST5 measures pull directly from earlier crowds, so it captures stars, brands and rivalries without anyone defining "star". If ST5 passes, ST1 only needs to beat it; if ST5 can't be built (no league-wide attendance), ST1 becomes the main test.
3. **MLB promotions by type (P1).** Data already confirmed; decades of MLB studies agree on direction and that bobbleheads lead. It also tells you whether promotions are worth the harder job in other leagues.

**Start in parallel, because it costs a season to wait:** begin saving each team's promo page the week before each game (P2) and the week-before weather forecast (O1). Before relying on archives, have Claude Code run a one-off check of how many of the five cities' teams have usable Wayback captures of their promo pages for 2022–2025 (many team sites render with JavaScript, so captures may be empty — not checked). If coverage is good, P2 moves to "test now".

**Next after the first three:** rare visit (R7) and same-city pair (R1), both derived and cheap; then page-view attention (A1), the owner's social-attention idea in its only free, allowed, historical form.
