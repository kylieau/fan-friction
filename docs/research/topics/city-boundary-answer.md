# Where a city's crowd competition ends

Research answer to the Fan/Friction brief dated October 22, 2026. Research checked October 22, 2026, Pacific time.

Status: closed, built as the drive-time rule (`docs/product/product-decisions.md`, "Where a city ends"; `scripts/further-out-drive.mjs`; downtowns in `src/config/metros.ts`), Oct 23, 2026. Open: one room counting for two cities.

## 1. The recommended rule

**Count a venue inside a city's boundary when it is within a normal, non-event drive of 60 minutes of its fixed downtown reference point; for an explicitly combined city, use the union of the catchments around its constituent downtowns.** Let neighboring cities' boundaries overlap: a venue can compete for both cities' audiences, even when it belongs to a different municipality, county or statistical metro. Put venues outside that boundary but within approximately two hours in **Further out**, with a stated reference-only tolerance of 15 minutes; anything farther away is **neither**.

**The numerical cutoffs are estimated policy choices, not official definitions or measured laws of audience behavior.** My recommendation is a conservative, reproducible screen for ordinary same-evening competition. It does not claim that competition disappears at the boundary.

**The immediate LA answer is to bring Toyota Arena in Ontario inside.** Its published drive estimate from Los Angeles is **43 minutes, reported**, substantially below the proposed cutoff. Removing Ontario solely because it crosses a county line is difficult to defend while counting all of Ventura County. The arena itself states that it accommodates **over 11,000 guests, official**, and identifies its location on the regional freeway network. [Reported route](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Los-Angeles/Toyota-Arena); [official arena description](https://www.toyota-arena.com/arena-info/about).

Three findings give the recommendation its practical limits:

- **Actual LA event-goers:** the LA Phil impact report's Hollywood Bowl table gives **54% City of Los Angeles, 19% elsewhere in LA County, 16% elsewhere in California and 11% outside California, official venue-supplied figures**. This supports a concentrated local audience with a wider tail. It does **not** separate Orange, Ventura, Riverside and San Bernardino counties, and does not establish Toyota Arena's crossover. The published table, rather than the report's less precise surrounding prose, is the basis here. [Report, Table 10](https://lachamber.com/clientuploads/pdf/2025/LA_Phil_Economic_Fiscal_Social_Impact_Report_2025.pdf?_t=1757095149).
- **Actual cross-city buying:** TD Coliseum's promoter deck gives **31% Greater Toronto Area, 27% Greater Hamilton Area and 58% combined, official venue claims**. This is evidence against treating Toronto–Hamilton separation as proof of separate audiences. However, only the search-indexed extract was inspectable: the full PDF exceeded the browsing tool's size limit. Its sample period and definitions were not verified. [Venue promoter deck, market slide](https://www.tdcoliseum.com/assets/doc/TDC-Promoter-Deck-2026-9448dd079d.pdf).
- **The long tail is real:** a report quoting a team representative puts **7% of 49ers season-ticket holders and 8% of single-ticket buyers in the Sacramento area, reported**. That establishes some travel across markets; it does not establish that Sacramento and the Bay Area should be one daily crowd boundary. [Comstock's reporting](https://www.comstocksmag.com/longreads/battling-bucks).

**Why this cutoff rather than a longer one?** The event-planning evidence supplies a defensible starting scale around an hour. It does not identify a universal optimum. I choose the shorter boundary to emphasize routine local competition; extending it to **90 minutes or 120 minutes, estimated alternative policies**, would admit substantially more satellite markets without evidence that their ordinary audiences overlap enough to justify that expansion. This is a judgment under incomplete evidence, with **medium confidence in the approach and low confidence in the exact cutoff**.

### How to make the rule unambiguous

The following are **estimated methodological choices**:

1. **Measure the venue entrance, not its municipality's center.** A town can straddle the line. A county is never added or removed wholesale merely because its county seat falls on one side.
2. **Fix the downtown points.** Use the downtown city hall/civic center as the neutral reference. For this brief's combined markets use San Francisco–Oakland–San Jose; Dallas–Fort Worth; Raleigh–Durham; Minneapolis–St. Paul; and Ottawa–Gatineau. Use the minimum travel time to those fixed points. Other cities, including LA, retain one downtown reference. This makes the choice visible; it is not evidence that LA has only one real activity center.
3. **Do not let additions become new centers.** Including Ontario does not give Ontario a new outward catchment. The same applies to every satellite admitted elsewhere.
4. **Use a stable travel baseline.** Prefer typical weekday midday driving, excluding unusual incidents and event queues. Event-night congestion changes the friction assessment, not permanent membership. Include routine bridge, border and ferry access time where applicable; a map's road-only time is insufficient for those crossings.
5. **Inside:** travel time at or below **60 minutes, estimated policy**. **Further out:** above that and at or below **135 minutes, estimated policy**. The latter is the brief's approximate two-hour reference band with **15 minutes of explicit tolerance, estimated policy**; it never increases the verdict boundary. **Neither:** above that reference ceiling.
6. **Allow geographic overlap.** A venue may be inside two neighboring cities' catchments. Municipal identity and audience competition answer different questions. Do not force a nearest-city allocation that discards evidence of a shared audience.

**Important distinction:** the routing figures below are **reported static car estimates**, principally from Rome2Rio. They are screening proxies, not measured midday medians or a computed set of civic-center-to-entrance isochrones. Classifications are therefore **estimated applications of the rule**. Near-threshold cases need entrance-level routing before a final inventory change. The report does not represent those checks as already completed.

### The nearby-city and multicenter cases, explicitly

- **Bay Area:** one combined market with fixed San Francisco, Oakland and San Jose downtowns. Each venue needs to pass only one of those catchments. A newly admitted Napa or Santa Cruz venue does not become another center.
- **Dallas–Fort Worth:** the union of both downtown catchments, not a circle around Dallas and not the four current counties. The same method applies to **Raleigh–Durham**, where the Burlington comparison above shows why the second center matters.
- **Los Angeles–San Diego:** test each actual venue to each downtown independently. Neither city automatically inherits the other's metro. Ontario clearly passes the LA screen; Temecula's published downtown San Diego route is just outside the cutoff, so it requires entrance-level verification rather than a categorical claim that all Temecula rooms belong to either city.
- **Washington–Baltimore:** the static screen includes central Baltimore from DC. That is a real implication, not an accidental county addition. If Baltimore were covered, the two boundaries could overlap. Confidence that this represents ordinary all-event substitution is lower than confidence in the road connection.
- **Toronto–Hamilton:** the screen tentatively includes Hamilton in Toronto's competition boundary, supported by disclosed GTA ticket buying. Keeping Hamilton as a separate covered city is compatible with this overlap. Excluding it automatically because it has its own CMA would discard the best audience-origin evidence found for this pair.

## 4. Confidence, open questions and thin evidence

### What I would treat as supported now

**Ontario should not remain Further out solely because of its county.** The venue-specific route and substantial distance below the policy threshold make its inclusion the strongest individual recommendation. This is **medium confidence** as an audience inference, not a claim to have measured the share of downtown LA fans it diverts.

**Whole-county consistency must work in both directions.** If accessible out-of-county venues come in, remote parts of today's included counties must be eligible to go out. LA/Ventura, Phoenix/Maricopa and Sacramento/El Dorado show the inconsistency especially clearly. Geography should be applied to venues, not to county seats.

**Composite markets need multiple fixed centers, and nearby covered cities can have overlapping audiences.** These are conceptual consequences of the question being asked, rather than extra statistical definitions.

### Where the research does not justify a final boundary change

1. **Most venue audience origins remain unavailable.** No usable public buyer-origin distribution was found for Toyota Arena, Glen Helen, the requested Riverside-area venues or most other edge rooms. Arena affiliations, “serving Southern California” language, and economic-output totals were not treated as origin studies. The LA Phil data are geographically too aggregated to fill this gap.
2. **The full inventory difference is unresolved.** The brief lacks the researched county lists for San Diego, Seattle, Salt Lake City and St. Louis, as well as several other metro definitions and the full Chicago collar-town list. It does not provide the venue inventory or exact edge-room addresses. The register above covers every named city, but cannot certify every county intersection or every changed room. An exhaustive difference claim would exceed the available evidence.
3. **The exact travel boundaries have not been computed.** The linked pages are city-to-place screening routes. None proves the median weekday midday trip from the chosen civic point to a particular entrance. Near-threshold results—especially San Bernardino, Riverside, Glen Helen, outer Orange County, Hamilton, Abbotsford and Toluca—are **low confidence** until that distinction is resolved. Ontario is less sensitive because its venue-specific estimate is comfortably shorter.
4. **Center selection is a remaining substantive choice.** The rule fixes the combined markets already named in the brief; it does not automatically turn every polycentric MSA into a collection of new anchors. LA has important centers besides downtown, and Miami has another major center in Fort Lauderdale. Adding them would expand the result. The public data reviewed do not establish one universal method for selecting those extra centers. This is a limitation of the proposed common rule, not something hidden in a county list.
5. **The cutoff is a policy approximation.** The Cardiff evidence shows that an hour is used in arena catchment work; it does not prove that two venues an hour apart share a specified fraction of buyers. Nor does a long trip imply no competition: destination concerts and infrequent sports visits can reach much farther. There is no researched basis here for calling the numerical cutoff scientifically optimal.
6. **Traffic and audience competition are related but different.** An out-of-boundary event may still load a shared freeway; two inside events may use different approaches or attract different audiences. No origin–destination traffic model was obtained for all the covered cities. Under the brief's rule that Further out never counts, such effects are deliberately truncated rather than proved absent.
7. **Cross-border and water routes need special care.** Windsor cannot be resolved from a short Detroit road distance without routine border processing. Victoria cannot be resolved from straight-line proximity without ferry access and waiting. The same principle applies to island and mountain access within otherwise included counties.
8. **Capacity remains independent of geography.** This is a boundary study, not a verified list of rooms meeting the brief's size threshold. For example, the venue's published concert setup for Pechanga Summit is **3,100 seats, official**, while an older ballroom capacity chart offers **5,573 in theater layout, official**. The applicable event configuration matters; neither a town's inclusion nor a maximum generic floor-plan capacity automatically settles the brief's **5,000-seat threshold, user-specified**. [Current concert venue page](https://pechanga.com/entertain/venues/pechanga-summit); [ballroom capacity chart](https://www.pechanga.com/uploads/assets/CapacityChart_01_web.pdf).

### Source access and evidence labels

**Official** identifies a statistics/public agency, team, league or venue's own information, including venue-supplied data reproduced in a commissioned report. **Reported** identifies journalism, consultants or third-party routing. **Estimated** identifies this report's classifications, methodological choices and geographic inferences. Current-boundary descriptions and the size threshold are supplied by the user, not independently verified official figures.

The TD Coliseum promoter PDF could not be fully loaded because it exceeded the browsing tool's size limit; only its indexed market extract was used and is explicitly qualified. A City of Santa Clara stadium transport document returned a forbidden-access response; it was not used to infer Bay Area buyer shares. Direct route pages for an obsolete San Manuel Amphitheater name and for the attempted Pittsburgh–Uniontown, PA address did not load; no bypass was attempted, and no conclusion relies on them. Glen Helen's current-name route was independently available. Where searches returned a same-named city in the wrong state or country, those results were discarded.

**Overall confidence:** medium in using accessible event geography instead of political/statistical labels; medium in Ontario's inclusion; low to medium in the other screened city changes; low in the exact universal cutoff and in any claim that the supplied notes permit a complete inventory migration.
