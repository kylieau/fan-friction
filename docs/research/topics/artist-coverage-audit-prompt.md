# Research brief: the first coverage audit (Los Angeles, New York, Mexico City)

Status: open. Written Oct 19, 2026 (evening) by Claude Code, from Kylie's Oct 19, 2026 answer 10 to the artist-genres proposal ("the first coverage audit: Los Angeles, New York and Mexico City, 100 listings each over one four-week window, measured against each city's venue table; yes, when the next research slot comes"). ## What this is for
Fan/Friction is a personal log of live events (sports and concerts), with a friction read for each date in a city. The app pre-lists events from about 1,000 people up and counts only events of 5,000+ toward the read (Kylie, Oct 6 and Oct 9, 2026). Its listings come from ticket sellers' public feeds: Ticketmaster's Discovery feed today, SeatGeek when its client id arrives; the league and team schedules come from the leagues' own public pages. Nobody has measured how much of what actually happens in a city's rooms those feeds see, how often they name the act rather than the show, or how good their genres are. Before any coverage number becomes a claim in the product, this audit measures it in three cities. Mexico City is in for the Latin detail: regional Mexican, Latin pop and local traditions are where a seller's broad "Latin" label hides the most.

**This is measurement, not a recommendation on sources.** The verdict is a set of numbers with their denominators. Say what would close the biggest gap at the end, in one section, nothing more.

## The three cities
Los Angeles (Los Angeles, Orange and Ventura counties), New York (the five boroughs, Long Island, Westchester, and North and Central Jersey), Mexico City (CDMX plus the connected State of Mexico municipalities). Use the same four-week window for all three: the first four full weeks (Monday to Sunday) that start after the day you begin, and write the dates at the top of the answer.

## How to build the sample
1. **The reference set first, from the rooms, not the sellers.** For every room in the city's table below, open the room's own calendar (its site, or the operator's) and list every public ticketed event in the window: concerts, games, comedy, theater and touring shows, family shows, festivals, boxing and wrestling, esports, anything a person buys a ticket for. A seller-only sample cannot measure the events the seller misses, so the room's own calendar is the denominator. Where a room has no calendar of its own, say so and use the operator's or the resident team's page. For Mexico City, where the table holds only the 5,000+ rooms, also list the 1,000–5,000 rooms you find hosting ticketed events in the window (Teatro Metropólitan, Auditorio BB, Pabellón Cuervo, Frontón México, Lunario, Teatro de la Ciudad, El Plaza Condesa and the like) and put them in a separate "rooms the app lacks" table with the fields the venue research used (name, area, capacity by setup, source).
2. **Then sample 100 events per city** from that reference set, stratified: about half at rooms of 5,000+ and half at 1,000–5,000; every format present in the window; some from independent promoters and venue-only sellers on purpose (AXS, DICE, Fever, a box office, Boletia, Superboletos, Eticket in Mexico). Write down the sampling rule you used and the counts per stratum.
3. **For each sampled event, record** (one row): room; date; start time (doors and stage if both exist); the act or production as the room's calendar names it; tour or production name if any; support acts; the seller or sellers that list it (Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, AXS, DICE, Fever, Boletia, Superboletos, Eticket, venue box office only, other); whether Ticketmaster names the act as an attraction or only in the listing's title; the seller's genre, first and second level, as written; the room's capacity for that setup; the size band (1,000–5,000, 5,000+, unknown).

## What to measure
Report each as a count over a denominator, never a bare percentage, by city, and split by size band and by format where the split changes the answer.
1. **Discovery coverage.** Of the reference events: how many appear on Ticketmaster; on SeatGeek; on any seller with a public feed or API; on a venue-only or promoter-only seller. Which formats and which rooms the app's feeds miss most.
2. **Identity.** Of the events a seller lists: how many name the performer, team or production as a distinct act (Ticketmaster's "attraction") and how many only in the title; how many act names match the room's calendar exactly, match through an alias, script or spelling, or do not match; collaborations, DJ personas, tribute acts and touring companies that would mislead a name match.
3. **Genre.** Of the events a seller lists: how many carry a first-level genre; how many of those map onto the app's eighteen categories (below); how many carry a specific second level, and how many of those second levels are right against the act's own material; how often the second level is the seller's default ("Other", "Undefined", a repeat of the first level). For Mexico City, how often "Latin" stands where regional Mexican, Latin pop, rock en español, cumbia, banda, corridos or reggaetón would be the honest label, and whether a Spanish-language source carries the finer label.
4. **Tour titles.** Where the act has a named tour or production in the window, how often the seller's title carries it, misses it, or carries a different one. Give the pairs.
5. **Unknown sizes.** How many sampled events have no capacity or attendance figure from any public source at the 1,000 line and at the 5,000 line, by room kind.
6. **Duplicates.** How often the same event appears twice (two sellers, two listings, a rescheduled date, a parent and a package), and what tells the pair apart.
7. **Effort.** How many hours the reference set and the sample took per city, and which step cost the most.

## The app's eighteen genre categories
Fifteen music categories plus three others, each the first level a seller's genre is mapped onto (Kylie, Oct 19, 2026): Rock (the seller's Alternative reads Rock), Pop, Hip-hop, R&B, Latin, Country, Electronic (Dance reads Electronic), Jazz, Classical, Metal, Reggae, Folk, Blues, Global (the seller's World), Faith (the seller's Religious); Comedy, Theater, Family. A seller's Other or Undefined becomes plain "Music". Count a second level only when it is finer than these.

## Rules
- **Label every number:** official, reported or estimated; and every share as a count over its denominator.
- Public pages only. Do not sign up, log in, buy anything, use a key, or bypass a block. If a page will not open, list it and move on; do not use a cached or archived copy.
- Use Spanish-language sources for Mexico City and say which ones.
- Where sources disagree (two capacities, two start times), give both and say which you would use and why.
- Never invent a Western genre for a tradition the sellers leave unlabeled; "unknown" is an answer.
- No costs. 🚩 Flag anything that would cost money or need a credit card (a seller's partner feed, a paid dataset).

## Output
1. The window's dates and the sampling rule, with counts per stratum per city.
2. One table per city with the 100 rows and the fields above.
3. A summary table: city × measure, each cell a count over a denominator, with the size-band and format splits that matter.
4. Identity and genre findings in prose, with the pairs that would mislead a name match and the Latin labels Mexico City's sellers flatten.
5. Rooms the app lacks (Mexico City especially), in the venue research's fields.
6. One short section: what single source or rule would close the biggest gap in each city, and whether a "good enough" line is reachable with the sellers the app reads today, with the number.
