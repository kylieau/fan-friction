## Reading the evidence

- **Announced:** the organisation or venue itself published the figure. A company filing is announced even when hosted by a regulator.
- **Reported:** a named trade publisher or research body published or compiled it. Trade-body aggregates are labelled reported; company statements remain announced.
- **Estimated:** arithmetic performed for this brief. These are derived historical ratios or averages, never observed event counts or proposed universal defaults.
- **D** means the denominator for the relevant performance: available ticket inventory, a named configuration, or an explicitly identified maximum. A maximum-based ratio is not the same as a sell-through ratio.
- Tickets sold, total ticket attendance, and physical people in the building are different measures. None of the ticket datasets below establishes a universal no-show adjustment. No adjustment is invented. Free lap children can also be absent from ticket totals; Disney on Ice explicitly permits some unticketed lap children [F3].
- Blank share / low / high cells are intentional. No arbitrary range or confidence interval is supplied. A historical minimum–maximum across examples would describe only those selected examples, not an event prediction interval.

## 2. Half-house and configuration findings

A useful decomposition is:

`paid tickets / published maximum = (offered inventory / published maximum) × (paid tickets / offered inventory)`

A theatre-mode capacity tells us only the first factor. A sell-through percentage with no numeric offered inventory does not identify the first factor. Neither supplies a universal “half-house” multiplier.

**Pandemic and older data:** No pre-2020 attendance figure is recommended as a current fill estimate. The older Broadway methodology date explains numerator definitions only. OPERA America's FY2022 report explicitly discusses cancellations and altered seasons; its multiyear averages span pandemic disruption and are not current baselines. League season detail marks shutdown/shortened seasons; these were not blended into the recent season comparison. Orchestras Canada explicitly identifies the pandemic disruption in its historical comparisons. Even later totals can reflect changed performance counts and repertoire, rather than a simple recovery in fill.

**Search limitations:** This research did not use paywalled bodies, resale prices, ticket-inventory scraping, private box-office systems, purchased reports or personal-data submissions. Search hits were treated as leads. Access failures are stated rather than filled with recalled numbers. No exhaustive audit of every company, city agency or public-record archive was possible; the absence finding is about a defensible broadly applicable table in the inspected evidence.

## 5. Recommendation

Leave every general category × requested room-size-band share blank for now and use the proposed **“Up to N”** ceiling fallback where the existing hierarchy has no count or sellout. None of the inspected evidence supports a transferable percentage for these categories across the app's US, Canadian and Mexican markets, particularly in larger rooms. Broadway's overall recent percentages describe NYC available-seat totals; opera budget cohorts, individual orchestra averages, Québec's mixed-discipline inventory, resident Christmas productions and selected boxscores have different scopes and denominators. The specific historical sales and inventory ratios above are useful evidence about their named engagements, with prospective averages remaining **Estimated**; they do not supply a general rule or an honest event prediction range. This recommendation concerns the displayed crowd figure only and does not change which events count toward the Verdict.
