CONTENT DESIGN REVIEW · 14 OCTOBER 2026

Fan/Friction: the names need a system

The naming system is not reliable enough. It hides known event types, makes some neutral and viewing events sound like home games, and lets title parsing corrupt identities. Fix misleading facts first; then unify identity, qualifiers and compact labels. Cosmetic casing is the smallest problem.

Sports fans should not have to decode every playoff initialism, and concertgoers should not have to learn the database’s catch-all categories. More abbreviations will not solve a 112px chip.

Proposed naming style guide — 10 rules

  1. Use a controlled, source-independent Type vocabulary. Show a verified sport or event format; use Sports event or Event for genuinely unknown records. Never expose raw keys, Undefined or Special.
  2. Lead discovery titles with the recognizable identity: teams for games, artist for a single-headliner concert, and official event/production name for festivals, bowls without known teams, theatre, fairs, conventions and other named events. Keep the same identity on chip, row and page.
  3. Build sports titles from verified participants and site roles: visitor at host for a home game; A vs B at a neutral site or when home/away is unknown. At must never be inferred from provider title order. Prefer designated visitor first at neutral sites when known.
  4. Use one generated title structure: Identity · distinguishing detail. Artist · Tour; Matchup · Round, Game N; Event · Day/session. Use at and vs without a period in generated titles. Preserve punctuation inside verified official names.
  5. Keep types and generic qualifiers in sentence case; preserve official artist, team, event and production styling. Normalize display labels across feeds and manual choices; protect names such as KATSEYE, $UICIDEBOY$ and UB40.
  6. Put readable sport/squad labels beside college identities whenever needed for understanding, not only collisions. Treat leagues and gender/squad as separate metadata. Apply men’s/women’s distinctions symmetrically.
  7. Use full round names and Game N on event pages. Compact sports badges may use G1 and established league/series abbreviations only with readable expansion in the row, preview or page. Normalize equivalent round terms while retaining verified league-specific names.
  8. Keep attendance-changing distinctions visible: preseason, spring training, doubleheader game, local date/time, day/weekend/session, pass scope and viewing format. Add year or city when it actually distinguishes an edition/site. Show conditional-game status prominently outside the name.
  9. Use verified short names on chips and protect essential distinguishing badges. For real collisions add the smallest useful qualifier: sport/squad, time, short venue, opponent, day or session. Deduplicate first. Test actual width and text scaling; expose the full title in accessible text and the page.
  10. Prefer structured facts over title parsing. Do not invent participants, home/away, genre, billing roles, day, game number or format. Preserve the full original title when structure is uncertain; suggest rather than silently rewrite personal log text.
