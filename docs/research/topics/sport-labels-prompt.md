# Research brief: short sport labels that name men's and women's alike

Status: open. Written Oct 15, 2026. For any research model that can browse.

## What this is for
Fan/Friction is a personal log of live events (sports and concerts) across 40 metros in the US, Canada and Mexico, with more countries later. Each game's title is the matchup, "Away at Home". When a school or club fields both a men's and a women's team in the same sport, the title also needs a short label saying which.

**The rule (Kylie, Oct 15, 2026):** "it is not okay that men's sports doesn't specify gender but women's does." Wherever a women's side is named, the men's side is named too. College basketball now reads **MBB** and **WBB** ("California at UCLA · MBB", "Pepperdine at UCLA · WBB"). She would like the same kind of shorthand for **every** sport, and wonders whether the label belongs in parentheses after the title: "Cal at UCLA (WBB)".

How the app shows labels today, for context:
- **Event labels (Kylie, Oct 15, "labels round 3"):** one label per event, the stage first (e.g. "NLCS Game 3", "Home opener", "Preseason"). In lists it sits on its own line under the title, without parentheses; inline (map bars, sentences) it goes in parentheses: "Brewers at Dodgers (NLCS G3)".
- **College sport labels** come after a middle dot today: "California at UCLA · MBB". A tournament game can carry both: "Sacramento St at UCLA · WBB (First round)".
- **Chips** on the map are 16 characters or fewer. They show a squad code only when two events on the same day would otherwise read the same.

This is research only. Don't present anything as decided; give options with evidence, and a recommendation she can react to.

## Questions

### 1. The codes: what's established, sport by sport
For every sport that has men's and women's sides at the college level (NCAA D-I/II/III, NAIA, junior college, U Sports in Canada), find the abbreviations already in wide use, and say where: athletic department sites (many run on Sidearm Sports, which uses codes in its URLs), NCAA stats and championships pages, sports information directors, conference sites, student papers, ESPN and other score sites, and everyday fan shorthand. Cover at least basketball, volleyball, soccer, ice hockey, lacrosse, water polo, tennis, golf, swimming and diving, track and field, cross country, gymnastics, fencing, rowing, beach volleyball, rugby and wrestling. Also cover the sports usually played by one side only, or with different names (baseball and softball, football, field hockey, women's flag football), and say whether they need a label at all.

For each sport give: men's code, women's code, alternatives seen, how common each is (with sources), and whether the pair is symmetric (both sides marked). Flag any established code that leaves the men's side unmarked (e.g. "BB" and "WBB").

### 2. Other levels and countries
- **High school:** boys/girls codes (e.g. BBB/GBB, "Varsity Boys Basketball") as state associations and MaxPreps write them.
- **Pro and national teams:** when the league name already says it (WNBA, NWSL, PWHL, WSL), is a label redundant? How is a national team shown (USWNT and USMNT; "England Women", "Lionesses")? How do apps label a club that fields both (e.g. Barcelona and Barcelona Femení)?
- **Outside the US:** UK university sport (BUCS), Australian and European clubs, Mexico (Liga MX Femenil). Is there a shared convention, or is it words ("Women's")?
- **Mixed and open events:** how they are labeled.

### 3. Where the label goes: patterns from apps people already use
Look at how leading apps and sites show the sport or gender next to a matchup: the ESPN app, The Athletic, Apple Sports, Google's sports cards, Yahoo Sports, CBS Sports, FotMob, Sofascore, OneFootball, school athletics sites, ticketing (Ticketmaster, SeatGeek), and calendar exports. Compare these forms, with screenshots or links:
- after a middle dot: "Cal at UCLA · WBB"
- in parentheses: "Cal at UCLA (WBB)"
- as a prefix: "WBB: Cal at UCLA"
- inside the team name: "UCLA WBB", "UCLA Women"
- on its own line under the title, or as a colored tag or chip

Then the hard case: a game with both a sport label and a stage label. How do these apps show "women's basketball, NCAA first round"? Options to compare: "Cal at UCLA (WBB · First round)", "Cal at UCLA · WBB (First round)", the label line "WBB · First round", or the stage only, with the sport moving elsewhere.

### 4. Will people understand the codes?
- Do casual fans read MBB / WBB / MSOC / WVB, or are words clearer ("Men's basketball")? Any evidence: usability writing, app reviews, how widely each code appears outside athletic departments.
- Screen readers: does "WBB" read as letters? How do accessible sites handle codes (e.g. a spoken label "women's basketball" behind the visible code)?
- Space: at a phone width of about 32 characters for a title, what fits?

### 5. A recommendation to react to
Two tables:
1. **The code list:** one row per sport, the men's and women's codes, and your confidence.
2. **The format:** three or four complete options, each written out at three lengths: chip (≤ 16 characters), list title (≤ 32), full title. For each option, show these games:
   - Cal at UCLA, women's basketball, regular season
   - the same game in the NCAA first round
   - UCLA at USC, men's volleyball
   - Stanford at UCLA, women's soccer
   - Dodgers at Angels (pro, no label needed)
   - a high school girls' basketball game

Say which option you would pick and why, and what each costs: line length, consistency with the app's existing label rules, and understanding by a casual fan.
