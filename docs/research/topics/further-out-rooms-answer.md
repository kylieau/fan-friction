### Evidence and interpretation

- **Official** means a venue, owner, operator, team, university, public agency, or its published release supplies the figure. It does not necessarily mean a fire-code limit.
- **Reported** means a secondary publication or mapping source supplies the figure. **Estimated** identifies an approximation, including operator estimates; an old figure is dated rather than presented as a newly measured capacity.
- Every capacity, driving duration, coordinate pair, and numerical event frequency is labeled. Calendar dates and years identify the relevant period, not measurements.
- Capacities are people per event or setup, never cumulative festival attendance. An attendance record is not substituted for capacity. Spaces in the same complex are not added together to clear the threshold.
- Rows descend by the recommended largest documented usable setup. Where that maximum is approximate or disputed, the ordering is approximate too. Smaller setups are retained when their room also has a qualifying setup.
- A recurring festival with several separately programmed ticketed days counts as a regular event site. A permit for a dormant or merely possible festival layout does not, by itself, qualify a separate site.
- **Driving limitation:** the linked route planners publish city-to-city or city-to-venue estimates, not a consistently verifiable City Hall departure. The table's **estimated downtown ranges** adapt those published routes for local access and a downtown departure: Lower Manhattan for New York, the Loop for Chicago, Center City for Philadelphia, downtown Milwaukee, downtown Dallas (with Fort Worth where separately sourced), downtown Houston, downtown Washington DC, and downtown Austin for shared relationships. They exclude event parking queues and exceptional traffic. The underlying planner duration is preserved beside the range so the estimate is auditable. These are approximate screening times, not independently verified traffic-free door-to-door routes. Borderline sites need a precise downtown route before import.
- **Coordinates:** latitude precedes longitude, in decimal degrees. Published map points are **reported**; their classification as a point representing the building/grounds outline is **estimated**, unless the source explicitly identifies an outline. They are suitable for venue pins, not entrance navigation. No point is claimed to be a verified public door.
- **Name history:** “same name documented” means no intervening rename was confirmed in the sources consulted; it is not proof that every temporary promotional alias has been recovered. Unknown effective dates and unverified Ticketmaster names are explicitly marked.
- **Cost:** public research was free. 🚩 Attending the ticketed events, booking venue space, premium mapping services, and any paywalled reporting would cost money. No such purchase was made. Ticket links are evidence, not a recommendation to buy.

## New York

### Provisional outside boundary

Treat the five boroughs, Long Island, Westchester/Rockland/Putnam, and the New York–Newark metropolitan counties of northern and central New Jersey as New York's own area. This keeps Newark, East Rutherford, Elmont, Wantagh, Holmdel, Piscataway, and comparable suburban rooms out of this table.

For this batch, **Dutchess and Orange counties, the separately defined Connecticut metropolitan/planning regions around Bridgeport and New Haven, Mercer County in New Jersey, and the Lehigh Valley/Poconos/Reading metropolitan regions in Pennsylvania are treated as outside**. The distinction follows separate metropolitan areas rather than municipal limits or the much broader combined statistical area. New York State explicitly lists Dutchess and Orange in the separate Kiryas Joel–Poughkeepsie–Newburgh metropolitan area; see [New York State geography](https://dol.ny.gov/new-york-state-geography) and the [Census delineation-file explanation](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/metro-micro/about/delineation-files.html).

**App-dependent cases:** Fairfield County is often treated as part of greater New York; Mercer County is often included in a Philadelphia sports market. If Fan/Friction already covers either within its own city area, assign those rooms to **New York** or **Philadelphia**, respectively, and remove the corresponding Further out relationship. The regional rule used here is an explicit research assumption, not a verified app boundary.
