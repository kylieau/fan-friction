# Research brief: how many people are there on one day of a multi-day event

Status: open. Written Oct 16, 2026. For any research model that can browse.

## What this is for
Fan/Friction gives each night in a city a "friction" read: how many big crowds shared it, how close their start times were, and whether they hit the same roads. A crowd of 5,000+ on that day feeds the read, and every number in the app is labeled announced, reported or estimated.

Multi-day events are the hard case: music festivals, comic cons and fan conventions, trade shows and expos, county and state fairs, auto shows, race weekends. Organizers publish one figure for the whole event, and it can mean very different things:
- **Unique attendees:** each person counted once, however many days they came.
- **Passes or tickets sold:** a three-day pass is one ticket but one person in the crowd every day. Coachella sells about 125,000 passes per weekend, so roughly 125,000 people are there each day, not a third of that.
- **Cumulative admissions** (turnstile or badge scans, "person-days"): each person counted once per day they came. Only this kind divides cleanly by the number of days.
- Round marketing figures ("over 100,000 fans") whose kind isn't stated.

The app's rule today (Kylie, Oct 7 and Oct 16, 2026): a multi-day total becomes a per-day figure, labeled estimated, only when it is known to be cumulative admissions. A total of unclear kind stays unsized until there's a better rule. That leaves many big events with no size, so the read misses them. **We need a rule for turning each kind of published figure into a defensible per-day crowd.**

## Questions

### 1. What organizers actually publish
For each type below, find how organizers and the press usually report attendance, with real examples (event, year, figure, what the figure counts, source):
- Multi-day music festivals: weekend passes, single-day tickets, capacity caps (Coachella, Stagecoach, Lollapalooza, Austin City Limits, Outside Lands, Governors Ball, EDC Las Vegas, BeachLife, smaller two-day city festivals).
- Comic cons and fan conventions (San Diego Comic-Con, New York Comic Con, Anime Expo, WonderCon, C2E2).
- Trade shows and expos (NAMM, CES, auto shows: Los Angeles, New York, Chicago).
- County and state fairs (L.A. County Fair, OC Fair, State Fair of Texas, Minnesota State Fair), which often publish daily gate counts.
- Race weekends and street events (the Grand Prix of Long Beach, IndyCar and NASCAR weekends), which often publish a three-day total.

### 2. How to turn each kind into a day's crowd
For each kind of figure (unique attendees, passes sold, cumulative admissions, a capacity cap, a marketing round number), propose how to get a per-day crowd, and say how confident it can be. For example: a per-day capacity cap is the crowd on a sold-out day; cumulative admissions ÷ days, perhaps weighted toward Saturday; unique attendees × average days attended ÷ days. Find published day-by-day splits where they exist (fairs and some conventions publish them), to test how uneven days are (Friday vs. Saturday vs. Sunday).

### 3. A rule to react to
