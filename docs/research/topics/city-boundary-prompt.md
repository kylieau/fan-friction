# Research brief: where a city's crowd competition ends

Status: open. Written Oct 22, 2026 by Claude Code, at Kylie's request (Oct 22, 2026: "how about a quick research question in this vein to determine what is appropriate?"), after the Los Angeles coverage check asked whether Ontario belongs inside Los Angeles. For any research model that can browse.

## What this is for
Fan/Friction is a personal log of live events (sports and concerts). Each date in a covered city gets a "friction" verdict: how hard the big events that date (5,000+ seats) compete with each other for the same people, plus traffic. Each city has a boundary. Rooms **inside** it count toward the verdict. Rooms **outside** it but within about a 2-hour drive are listed as "Further out", for reference only, and never count.

Today each boundary was set city by city, usually by whoever researched that city's rooms (a list of counties, a metro area, a few named "edge" rooms). Nobody chose one rule for all of them. The question that started this: Los Angeles counts Los Angeles, Orange and Ventura counties. Ontario's 11,000-seat arena (San Bernardino County, about an hour from downtown) is Further out. The Coachella Valley (2½ hours) is now Further out too. Is Ontario on the right side of the line? And what rule should draw the line everywhere?

## The question
1. **What should decide whether a room is inside a city for this purpose?** "This purpose" means: would a big event there plausibly pull the same people as a big event downtown that night (fans who would otherwise attend, or traffic on the same roads)? Weigh the candidates, with evidence, and recommend one rule, or one rule with a stated exception:
   - official statistical areas (US Census MSA or CSA; Statistics Canada CMA; Mexico's INEGI metropolitan zones);
   - TV market (Nielsen DMA);
   - drive time from downtown (what cutoff, and why);
   - commuting flows;
   - where ticket buyers actually come from, for teams and venues that publish it (season-ticket or buyer ZIP studies, venue economic-impact reports);
   - team territories (league home-market definitions);
   - anything better you find.

   Say how the rule handles a city with several centers (the Bay Area's San Francisco, Oakland and San Jose; Dallas and Fort Worth; Raleigh and Durham), and two covered cities near each other (Los Angeles and San Diego; Washington and Baltimore if Baltimore were covered; Toronto and Hamilton).
2. **Apply the rule to Los Angeles first**, by name: the Inland Empire (Ontario, San Bernardino, Riverside, Temecula, Highland, Lake Elsinore, Perris), the Coachella Valley (Indio, Palm Desert, Indian Wells, Rancho Mirage), and the outer edges of Ventura and Orange counties. For each: inside, Further out, or neither, with the evidence.
3. **Then test it on every covered city** listed below. For each, give the boundary the rule produces and every place where it differs from today's boundary (a county that should come in or go out, a named edge room that no longer fits). Keep this to differences; do not re-list rooms.
4. **Say how confident you are**, and where the evidence was thin (most venues publish no buyer-origin data; say so rather than guess).
