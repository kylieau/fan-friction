# Cleveland — Venue & Crowd-Event Research for Fan/Friction

**Answer date:** 12 October 2026 (all "as of" dates below are the publication date of the linked source; facts were checked on 12 Oct 2026).
**Time zone for all events:** America/New_York (ET). **Local naming:** U.S. English; corporate naming rights change frequently (see rename columns).

**How to read this file.** Every numeric figure links directly to the page it came from. Labels: **official** = venue, team, league, university, government or organizer page; **reported** = news outlet, encyclopedia or trade publication; **resale/aggregator** = ticket-resale or event-aggregator site (low confidence, kept only to show conflicts). Capacity is never used as attendance. Where sources disagree and the disagreement could change a 1,000 / 5,000 classification, both values are kept. Most figures were read from search-result extracts of the linked pages; pages that were paywalled (e.g. Crain's Cleveland Business) were not bypassed and are flagged.

**Coordinates.** No fetched source supplied surveyed coordinates for these rooms, so coordinates are marked *not captured* and the sourced street address is given as the geocoding anchor. One exception (Geauga fairgrounds) comes from an aggregator and is labelled as such. This is a deliberate gap rather than an invented value.

## 1. Recommended boundary and uncertain edge venues

### Recommendation

Use the **five-county Cleveland core** — Cuyahoga, Geauga, Lake, Lorain and Medina — as the Cleveland city model. This is the long-standing federal definition of the Cleveland metro: the Cuyahoga County Planning Commission states the [Cleveland-Elyria MSA includes exactly these five counties](https://countyplanning.us/resources/census-data/population-estimates/2010-to-2019/). It matches the working search area and contains every major-league and minor-league tenant whose name says "Cleveland" or "Lake/Lorain".

Two boundary facts to record:

- **2023 federal change.** Under OMB Bulletin 23-01 the metro is now titled "Cleveland" and Ohio's labour-market office lists [Ashtabula County as "added"](https://prod-ohlmi.mt.gov/_docs/Maps/MetroMicro2025.pdf). No Ashtabula room around 1,000+ was verified in this pass, so adding it changes nothing today; re-check if a venue is found there.
- **Akron and Canton are separate federal metros.** The same OMB listing names [Akron and Canton-Massillon as their own MSAs](https://prod-ohlmi.mt.gov/_docs/Maps/MetroMicro2025.pdf), and the [Cleveland-Akron-Canton CSA](https://countyplanning.us/resources/census-data/population-estimates/2010-to-2019/) spans 14 counties. Treating the whole CSA as "Cleveland" would pull in two other cities' venue ecosystems, which the brief says not to do.

- **Huntington Bank Field ×2.** The lakefront stadium and the Brook Park dome will share the exact name. Match on address/municipality and date, never on name alone.
- **Old names still live in feeds:** Rocket Mortgage FieldHouse (and its old domain), Quicken Loans Arena, Canal Park, Classic Park, Crushers Stadium / All Pro Freight Stadium, FirstEnergy Stadium.
- **Multi-room grounds:** Public Auditorium (Public Hall vs Music Hall vs Little Theater); Playhouse Square (State, Connor Palace, Mimi Ohio, Allen, Hanna, smaller stages); Agora (Theatre vs Ballroom); House of Blues (Music Hall vs Cambridge Room); Severance (Mandel vs Reinberger); Blossom (pavilion vs lawn vs Porthouse Theatre on the grounds ([Case Western ECH](https://case.edu/ech/articles/b/blossom-music-center))).

- **Add-on listings:** Browns PSLs, suites and parking passes; Guardians resale via StubHub; fair grandstand tickets vs gate admission; Jacobs Pavilion/Nautica parking.
- **Split home sites:** Charge (Rocket Arena early season, Public Hall after); CSU (Wolstein, Woodling, Rocket Arena).
