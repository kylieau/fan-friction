# Cincinnati — event travel and city-type answer (Fan/Friction Gridlock read)

**Research date:** 13 October 2026. All sources are public web pages accessed on that date. Every numeric claim links to its source. Where no source was found the entry says **gap** and what was searched.

**Bottom line:** No measured attendee mode split exists in public sources for any Cincinnati venue or event. The only hard event-travel evidence is streetcar boardings, and those show rail carries a small slice of even the biggest downtown crowds. Recommended existing setting: **`sprawl` (driving)**. No measured car shares can be wired. Two sourced **estimates** (Riverbend campus, Cincinnati Open) can be wired; every other venue stays on the city default (0.85).

## 3. Ridership inference (method shown)

### 3a. Bengals home opener, Sunday 25 Sept 2016 (Paul Brown Stadium, now Paycor)

- Sunday streetcar boardings: **4,964**, against a **budgeted** Sunday of **990** ([WCPO](https://www.wcpo.com/news/local-news/hamilton-county/cincinnati/streetcar-had-more-than-18k-passengers-last-weekend)). The game was a 1 p.m. kickoff vs Denver with more than 65,000 expected; only the standard two streetcars ran ([WCPO](https://www.wcpo.com/news/local-news/hamilton-county/cincinnati/is-the-cincinnati-streetcar-ready-for-cincinnati-bengals-first-home-game)).
- Baseline subtraction: 4,964 − 990 = **3,974 excess boardings**. The baseline is a budget, not an observed count, so it is weak.
- Return trips: if each game rider boarded twice, that is about **1,987 people**; if once, up to 3,974.
- Denominator: capacity [65,515](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paycor_Stadium) (actual attendance for that game: **gap**).
- Result: streetcar users were at most about **3.0%** (two boardings) to **6.1%** (one boarding) of a full house. This is an upper bound: the same period's coverage also mentions crowds after a Reds game on a Sunday ([WCPO](https://www.wcpo.com/news/local-news/hamilton-county/cincinnati/watch-are-two-streetcars-on-the-weekends-enough-to-handle-crowds)); if the Reds were home that day the excess is shared.
- Car involvement: the streetcar loop runs only between OTR, downtown and The Banks ([City](https://www.cincinnati-oh.gov/streetcar/about-the-streetcar/ridership-records/)). Many riders parked on the loop. They are **car-involved**, not car-free.

### 3b. Event-day peaks since 2019 (no denominator possible)

- Best day of 2023: **8,747** boardings on Sat 16 Sept 2023; all-time best **14,670** during BLINK on 12 Oct 2019. The 2023 weekend daily average was **3,766** ([City ridership records](https://www.cincinnati-oh.gov/streetcar/about-the-streetcar/ridership-records/)). Excess on 16 Sept 2023 ≈ **4,981** boardings.
- 16 Sept 2023 fell inside Oktoberfest Zinzinnati, held 14–17 Sept on Fifth Street that year ([WKRC](https://www.local12.com/news/local/oktoberfest-zinzinnati-street-closures-traffic-changes-fifth-street-german-walnut-columbia-parkway-downtown-sixth-fourth-broadway-sycamore-sentinel-lawrence-main)). No same-day attendance exists, so no share can be computed.
- City staff named Bengals Monday Night Football in Sept 2023 and Oktoberfest as big ridership drivers ([WVXU](https://www.wvxu.org/local-news/2023-10-05/streetcar-breaks-annual-ridership-record-2023)); the city also credits Reds/Bengals games and a 2023 Paycor stadium concert ([City](https://www.cincinnati-oh.gov/dote/news/connector-reaches-new-ridership-milestone-1-millionth-passenger-in-a-single-calendar-year)). Neither gives per-event counts: **gap** (searched city news, CincyInsights links; daily data dashboard not machine-readable here).
- Counting method: automatic passenger counters; ambiguous boardings are counted as zero, so counts run low ([City](https://www.cincinnati-oh.gov/streetcar/about-the-streetcar/ridership-records/)).

**Conclusion of 3a–3b:** the only rail mode in the region carries low single digits of a stadium crowd at most, and much of that is park-and-ride. This is the strongest local evidence and it points away from `transit` (0.4).

### 3c. Comparison (dated, used only for context)

A TRB-era analysis of baseball parks (Shea, Cleveland's Jacobs Field, Anaheim) found that superior rail access can carry up to 40% of spectators, and that without good rail transit average vehicle occupancy is about 2.6 ([World Transit Research summary](https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/1091)). Cincinnati has no rapid rail; the streetcar is a 3.6-mile downtown loop.

## 5. Regional conclusion and city type (gap item 2)

**Recommendation: `sprawl` (driving).**

Rationale, using measured or operational evidence only:

1. The single measured event-travel signal (streetcar boardings) bounds rail at roughly 3–6% of a full Bengals house at most, and much of it is park-and-ride (§3a).
2. Regular Metro service is not boosted for big events; the city told Riverfest crowds Metro would run normally ([City 2022](https://www.cincinnati-oh.gov/cityofcincinnati/traffic-alerts/riverfest-street-closures/)).
3. The region's event operations are built around parking: 8,600+ Banks spaces ([Banks](https://thebankspublicpartnership.com/parking-at-the-banks/)), a 5,300-space zoning requirement at TQL ([SSD](https://soccerstadiumdigest.com/2018/11/fc-cincinnati-stadium-parking-plan-debated/)), parking bundled with Riverbend and Cincinnati Open tickets.
4. No rapid rail exists; Metro Rapid BRT only starts in late 2027 (§10).

**Strongest contrary evidence (why `hub` is arguable):** the four biggest downtown rooms (Paycor, GABP, Heritage Bank Center, TQL) form one tight cluster joined by a free streetcar, a pedestrian bridge from Newport reopened in June 2026 ([WCPO](https://www.wcpo.com/news/local-news/purple-people-bridge-reopening-today-ahead-of-schedule)), and shared garages. Crowds at The Banks share approaches and parking. That is a spill pattern, not a transit mode share.

**Practical note:** `sprawl` and `hub` carry the same fallback (0.85), so this choice changes only spill handling, not car share. `transit` (0.4) is contradicted by every piece of evidence found.

**Venue exceptions:** campus venues (Nippert, Fifth Third Arena, Cintas, Truist) probably have more walk-ins from students; not measured. Riverbend campus and Mason are near-total car.

### Weighted car shares

Capacity-weighted, using the largest single configuration per room (no mutually exclusive configurations added, no multi-day totals), for the seven rooms with a sourced capacity of 5,000+: Paycor 65,515, GABP 42,319, Nippert 38,088, TQL 26,000, Riverbend 20,500, Heritage Bank Center 17,556, Truist 10,000 = **219,978**.

| Scenario | Weighted car share | Note |
|---|---|---|
| As wired (Riverbend 0.97 estimated; all others default 0.85) | **0.861** | Only 9.3% of weight rests on any estimate; the rest is the default, so this is **not evidence** |
| Illustrative bounds (unknown rooms 0.80–0.97; Riverbend 0.92–0.99) | 0.811 – 0.972 | Judgment bounds, not measurements |

Excluded for missing capacity: Fifth Third Arena, Cintas Center, Cincinnati Open Center Court. **Typical-attendance weighting: not computed** — only the Reds have a sourced 2026 average ([24,589](https://local12.com/sports/reds/cincinnati-reds-2026-attendance-drop-great-american-ball-park-fans-losing-mlb-dodgers)); Bengals, FC Cincinnati, UC and arena averages are gaps.

**Do huge outlying venues obscure the downtown pattern?** No — the reverse. The downtown cluster holds 151,390 of 219,978 (68.8%) of the weighted capacity. The outlying rooms are smaller: Riverbend 9.3%, Truist 4.5%; Nippert (Uptown) 17.3%.

### Clusters, shared roads, shared audiences (separate findings)

- **Central Riverfront / The Banks:** Paycor, GABP, Heritage Bank Center, Brady Music Center, plus Riverfest and Oktoberfest. Shared: Banks garages sold to both Reds and Bengals ([Banks](https://thebankspublicpartnership.com/parking-at-the-banks/)); Second Street ramps (closed for the bridge project, §10); river bridges to Covington/Newport; TANK trolley; streetcar.
- **West End / OTR:** TQL Stadium; shared Central Parkway closures and streetcar ([City](https://www.cincinnati-oh.gov/cityofcincinnati/traffic-alerts/fcc-tql-stadium-gameday-traffic-road-closures/)).
- **UC campus (Uptown):** Nippert and Fifth Third Arena are neighbours ([Stadium Journey](https://stadiumjourney.com/stadiums/nippert-stadium-s456)).
- **Kellogg Avenue riverfront:** Riverbend, PNC Pavilion, the future Farmer Music Center, Belterra Park; all feed Kellogg Ave and I-275 Exit 72 ([WCPO](https://wcpo.com/news/transportation-development/move-up-cincinnati/cincinnati-police-encourage-riverbend-concert-goers-to-consider-alternative-routes)).
- **Shared audience:** UC football uses TQL for one 2026 game ([3D Digital Venue](https://3ddigitalvenue.com/3dmap/clients/college-football/nippert-stadium/)), so a UC crowd can land in the West End cluster.

No new distance rule or hard-access flag is proposed.
