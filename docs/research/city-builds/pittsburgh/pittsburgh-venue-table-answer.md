**How to read the labels**

- **Official** = venue, team, league, university, organizer or government page.
- **Reported** = news, tourism bureau, ticketing blog, or other secondary source.
- **Wiki** = Wikipedia, used only where no primary figure was found; treat as reported and re-confirm.
- **Derived** = arithmetic from figures in one cited source (method stated).
- **Unknown** = not found in a public source during this pass. No figure has been invented.
- **Proposal** = announced plan, not a confirmed or scheduled change.

Capacity is never used as attendance. Attendance figures appear only where a source published them, with the count type stated.

## 1. Recommended boundary

### Recommendation

Use the **current eight-county Pittsburgh Metropolitan Statistical Area**: Allegheny (core), plus Armstrong, Beaver, Butler, Fayette, **Lawrence**, Washington and Westmoreland.

Reasoning, with sources:

- The Office of Management and Budget's July 2023 delineation **added Lawrence County** to the Pittsburgh MSA, making it eight counties ([Business Journal Daily](https://businessjournaldaily.com/?p=202071); [TribLive](https://naviga.triblive.com/?p=3291302)). The addition rested on a 2021 standard letting an outlying county join when at least [25% of its residents commute](https://businessjournaldaily.com/?p=202071) into the metro, which is direct catchment evidence.
- The Bureau of Labor Statistics now uses the eight-county definition in its [May 2025 occupational release](https://www.bls.gov/regions/mid-atlantic/news-release/2026/occupationalemploymentandwages_pittsburgh_20260710.htm), and the University of Pittsburgh's regional data brief does the same as of [March 2026](https://ucsur.pitt.edu/sites/default/files/Census%20Reports/UCSUR_CensusEstimates_Brief_March262026.pdf).
- Your starting list omitted Lawrence. It should be added: it is officially in the metro, and the region's road-racing circuit sits on the Lawrence/Beaver line (see edge venues).
- Event-catchment evidence also supports the outer counties: the Big Butler Fair reports visitors from [Pennsylvania, Ohio, New York and West Virginia](https://visitpittsburgh.com/events/big-butler-fair), and a Washington County commissioner describes that county's fair as a [regional and interstate event](https://www.monvalleyindependent.com/2025/08/01/washington-county-offers-fair-preview). Cross-state draw does not by itself justify adding out-of-metro venues.

**Caution on legacy definitions:** at least one BLS data table still describes the [seven-county area](https://www.bls.gov/regions/mid-atlantic/data/xg-tables/ro3fx9529.htm) without Lawrence. Older pages using "seven counties" are describing the pre-2023 boundary.

### Outside scope (not researched for capacity in this pass)

Distant Pennsylvania markets (State College, Erie, Johnstown/Altoona), Ohio markets (Youngstown, Steubenville, Cleveland) and West Virginia (Morgantown, Wheeling) stay outside. Neighbouring non-metro Pennsylvania counties (Indiana, Greene, Somerset) are also outside. No event-catchment evidence was found during this pass that would justify promoting any of their venues. If you later want a catchment test, the most plausible candidates are university venues in Indiana County and motorsport in Greene County, but **no capacities were verified for them** and none are listed below.

### 5b. Duplicate and matching risks

**Same-name collisions (high risk for feed matching):**

- **"Highmark Stadium"**: Pittsburgh's stadium used this name until April 2026, while Highmark also holds naming rights for the Buffalo Bills' stadium ([StadiumDB](https://stadiumdb.com/designs/usa/riverhounds_stadium)). Historic Pittsburgh records must be matched on city plus venue ID, never name alone.
- **"Washington County Fair"**: Iowa ([Southeast Iowa Union](https://www.southeastiowaunion.com/washington/news/washington-county-fair-draws-crowds-with-lively-events/article_9aa31722-9da2-4513-aaf1-4cac4c68473a.html)) and Texas ([KWHI](https://kwhi.com/2025/09/22/washington-co-fair-reports-record-attendance-sale-figures/)) fairs with the same name ran in 2025–26, with different dates and counts. The Pennsylvania fair is officially the **Washington County Agricultural Fair** in Arden.
- **"Heinz"**: Heinz Field (now A01) vs Heinz Hall (A24); both appear in Pittsburgh listings.
- **"PNC Park" vs similarly named "PNC Field"** listings: seen mixed together on aggregator pages during this search. Match on city.
- **"UPMC Events Center"** (RMU, A18) vs **"UPMC Cooper Fieldhouse"** (Duquesne, A19): both UPMC-branded college arenas in Allegheny County.
- **Carnegie Music Hall**: besides Oakland (A27), Pittsburgh-area Carnegie halls exist in Braddock, Homestead, Carnegie and the North Side ([Post-Gazette](https://pge.post-gazette.com/.pf/showstory/202403220096/3)).

**Proximity and grouping risks:**

- **North Shore cluster:** A01, A02, A12 and A25 sit together; B01 (Draft) and B13 (plaza) overlap the same blocks. Platform groupings of "Stage AE" can hide which room was used ([Songkick](https://detour.songkick.com/venues/1152511-stage-ae) lists one venue at 5,500).
- **Lower Hill / Uptown cluster:** A04, A16 and A19 are adjacent. Penguins-affiliated and Live Nation listings may share parking or premium products.
- **Arts Landing and Downtown riverfront:** B03, B04, B06 and B12 reuse overlapping footprints; each needs its own record.
- **Convention center:** B02 expo and B05 convention both use David L. Lawrence Convention Center.

**Listing types that can double-count one event:** suites and club seats at A01 and A04; parking passes (Star Lake, North Shore lots); VIP/lawn upgrades at A03; separate grandstand tickets inside fairs (A11 inside B09; A14 inside B10); free mobile tickets for Steelers camp; premium club spaces added at A04 in 2023.

**Multi-tenant rooms:** A01 (Steelers and Pitt, different capacities); A13 (four teams); A15 (Wild Things and PennWest California baseball); A10 (Geneva and Beaver Falls HS).
