# Austin city-type answer: how people actually reach event venues

**Research date:** 11 October 2026 (web sources accessed this day). **Scope:** Austin–Round Rock–San Marcos metro, assessed against Fan/Friction's existing Gridlock city types (`sprawl` 0.85, `hub` 0.85, `transit` 0.40 fallback car share).

**Bottom line up front.** No public source found in this pass publishes a full, measured person mode split for any Austin event venue. Every car-share value below is therefore an *estimate* built from partial measured counts (rail boardings, parking supply, shuttle arrangements) and one out-of-region comparison. On that evidence Austin event travel is car-dominant everywhere, with the lowest plausible car shares at Austin City Limits Music Festival (ACL Fest) and SXSW and moderately reduced shares at the central UT/Q2 venues. `transit` (0.40) is clearly rejected. `sprawl` and `hub` share the same 0.85 fallback, so car-share evidence alone cannot choose between them; the choice rests on clustering and spill. This answer recommends **`hub`**, with `sprawl` as the strongest alternative.

---

## 1. Method and evidence labels

| Label | Meaning in this document |
|---|---|
| **Measured** | A published count from the event (e.g., rail riders on a match day). None of these is a full mode split. |
| **Projected** | A forecast or expectation published before the event. |
| **Estimated** | This analysis' transparent estimate, with assumptions, range and confidence. All car shares are in this category. |
| **Background** | Ordinary commuting data. Never used as an event mode share. |

Rules applied, following the brief: a car trip to remote parking or a park-and-ride followed by a bus/shuttle is counted as **car-involved**; rideshare/taxi is car; private bar/charter shuttles are counted as *unclassified* because the access leg is unknown; raw boardings are converted to people only with a stated round-trip assumption; multi-day totals are converted to per-day crowds and never treated as one simultaneous crowd.

The companion venue research referenced in the brief was **not available** to this pass, so venues were enumerated independently (section 4 lists gaps).

**Source conflict preserved:** the 2024 "about one-fifth of attendees" framing and the 2025 "830 event-day riders" average differ by roughly an order of magnitude. Likely causes are free-fare promotion, SXSW overlap, station-only vs systemwide counting, and unclear boarding definitions. This answer does not pick one; it carries the range.

#### SXSW 2026 incremental-ridership inference

CapMetro reported more than 7,500 additional rail customers and more than 3,000 additional bus riders over the Thursday–Sunday opening weekend versus a typical weekend ([CBS Austin](https://www.cbsaustin.com/news/local/capmetro-says-sxsw-opening-weekend-brought-7500-more-rail-riders-3000-more-bus-riders)). That is ≈10,500 extra boardings over 4 days ≈ 2,600 per day, or ≈1,300 people per day if each makes a round trip. Against a district crowd in the tens of thousands (conference badges alone 37,770 in 2025, per [Skift](https://meetings.skift.com/2026/03/11/sxsw-2026-under-pressure-to-deliver/)), incremental transit is plausibly 2–5%. The E2 value of 7% assumes some festival travel was already inside the "typical weekend" baseline. There is no public attendance denominator for the whole district, hence the very low confidence.

## 6. Clusters, shared roads and shared audiences (separate findings)

1. **UT campus cluster:** DKR and Moody Center are adjacent and share I-35 frontage, MLK, Dean Keeton, Red River and San Jacinto approaches. This is the clearest venue-to-venue spill case in the region.
2. **COTA site:** F1, Germania concerts and race-day Super Stage crowds share one site and entrance road. Treat them as one crowd during F1, never as additive venues.
3. **Downtown/Zilker:** ACL Fest, SXSW hubs and the marathon share downtown garages, MetroRapid 801/803 and the Red Line. These are mostly seasonally separate.
4. **North Burnet/Domain:** Q2 Stadium shares the Red Line and Burnet Road with The Domain. The new North Burnet/Uptown station (late 2027) adds a second nearby stop.
5. **Williamson suburbs:** H-E-B Center (183A) and Dell Diamond (SH-45/US-79) are self-contained, parking-led sites about 15 miles apart. Little evidence of shared approaches.
6. **Shared regional audience and capacity:** October stacks ACL and F1 (on separate weekends in 2025 and 2026) and possibly UT football on an F1 day (19 Oct 2024; F1 date not re-verified). These events share airport, hotel and SH-71/US-183 capacity rather than local venue approaches.

## 7. Final answers

### 7.1 Recommended existing city type: **`hub`** (moderate-low confidence)

**Rationale.** All evidence points to car-dominant event travel (regional fixed-venue estimate 0.77, range 0.64–0.89), which rules out `transit` (0.40). Because `sprawl` and `hub` share the 0.85 fallback, the decision turns on spill. Austin has a genuine central cluster of large co-located venues with shared approaches (UT campus) plus central open-site crowds (ACL, SXSW) where car shares are materially lower than 0.85. Its outlying venues are self-contained and parking-led and are best handled by venue overrides. "In between" describes that structure better than "driving".

**Strongest contrary evidence (for `sprawl`):**
- Even the best-served venue (Q2, which has a dedicated rail station) shows observed rail use of roughly 2–11% on typical days.
- No rail serves DKR, Moody Center or COTA.
- About 31% of fixed capacity sits in outlying, parking-included sites estimated at 0.88.
- The single largest crowd (F1) is about 85% car-involved.

If the model's `hub` spill logic assumes walkable inter-venue movement that Austin lacks outside the UT campus, `sprawl` is defensible.

**Venue exceptions that need overrides under either type:** E1 ACL (0.50), E2 SXSW (0.45), A1 DKR (0.70), A2b Moody basketball (0.72), A3 Q2 (0.75) below the fallback; A4b/A5/A6/A7/E4 (0.93–0.95) above it.

### 7.3 Boundary recommendation and inconsistencies between briefs

- **Recommended boundary:** the official five-county Austin–Round Rock–San Marcos MSA (Travis, Williamson, Hays, Bastrop, Caldwell; [Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/south-central-austin/real-estate/austin-area-continues-to-see-population-increase-falls-out-of-fastest-growing-metro-ranking/)). COTA sits in eastern Travis County near Bastrop County, and Bastrop County markets lodging to COTA race crowds ([Explore Bastrop County](https://explorebastropcounty.com/calendar-event/motogp-red-bull-grand-prix-of-the-americas-2026/)). Including Bastrop and Caldwell therefore matters for COTA access.
- **Keep separate:** Killeen–Temple, College Station and San Antonio. No audience or event-travel evidence was found to justify merging them.
- **Edge-case municipalities:**
  - San Marcos (in the MSA, but on the I-35 corridor toward San Antonio; Texas State audiences likely draw from both metros).
  - New Braunfels (San Antonio MSA, close to San Marcos).
  - Georgetown and Leander (in the MSA, at the commuter-rail edge).
  - Cedar Park (in the MSA; its transit-authority status was not verified in this pass).
  - Burnet County (outside the MSA but inside the City's six-county commuter survey).
- **Inconsistencies between briefs:**
  - The brief's starting area names three counties; the MSA has five.
  - The 2025 commuter survey uses six counties, including Burnet ([City of Austin](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=470807)).
  - The brief's starting lead list includes the Austin Convention Center, which is closed through 2028.
  - The companion venue answer referenced in the brief was not available, so row keys cannot be matched.

### 7.4 Data gaps, model limitations, source conflicts and inaccessible sources

- **No measured full mode split** for any Austin venue or event. No public travel surveys were found from Texas Athletics, Austin FC/Q2, Moody Center/Oak View Group, COTA or C3 Presents (ACL).
- **No shuttle ridership published** by COTA or ACL. No CapMetro game-day boardings found for DKR or Moody Center.
- **Capacity sources are partly secondary** (Wikipedia, ticket aggregators, a third-party PDF of the COTA calendar). Re-verify against venue sites.
- **Source conflicts:**
  - Q2 rail: about one-fifth of attendees (2024) vs ~830 event-day riders (2025).
  - McKalla cost is printed as $600 million in one Community Impact edition and $60 million elsewhere. $60 million is consistent across [KUT/Austin Monitor](https://austinmonitor.com/stories/2025/05/capmetros-red-line-station-near-the-domain-is-back-on-track-but-now-double-the-price/); the larger figure is treated as a typo.
  - F1: ">400,000" (Reuters) vs "~430,000" (GP Destinations), with no official 2025 figure.
  - Convention center reopening: "winter 2028" ([Community Impact, early 2025](https://communityimpact.com/south-central-austin/government/austin-convention-center-redevelopment-design-revealed/)) vs "early 2029".
  - I-35 downtown segment start: late 2026 vs 2027 (section 8).
  - Q2 capacity: 20,738 vs ~20,500 (a small definitional gap; ticketed average vs seat count).
- **Definitions:** Q2's 20,738 is a ticketed average, not a scanned count; F1 totals are three-day person-days; marathon numbers are registrants, not starters.
- **Model limitations:** a single regional type cannot represent a region whose estimated venue car shares span 0.45–0.95. Venue overrides carry most of the information. Rideshare is counted as car, but it produces two vehicle trips per drop-off, so a car *share* understates vehicle pressure at rideshare-heavy venues (Moody Center, ACL).
- **Inaccessible sources:** the Austin American-Statesman game-day rail article is on a paywalled reprint (PressReader) and was not used for figures. CapMetro's ridership dashboard and the ACL economic impact report were not retrieved in this pass. No access controls were bypassed.
