# Minneapolis–St. Paul: event-travel evidence and city-type assessment for Fan/Friction Gridlock

**Research date:** Monday, 12 October 2026. All sources are public and were read on this date. Figures from news sites that sit behind a subscription (mainly the Star Tribune) were read from publicly displayed excerpts only; they are flagged where used.

**Scope note:** The companion venue research referenced in the brief was **not available** to this pass. Venues were therefore enumerated independently, and every row below carries its own ID (`MSP-xx`) so it can be re-keyed to the companion answer later. Coverage gaps are listed in §6 and §9.

**Definitions used throughout**

- **Car-involved share** = share of *people* (not vehicles, not ticket buyers) whose trip to the event used a private car, carpool, taxi or rideshare for any leg. Per the brief, a drive to a remote lot followed by a shuttle counts as car-involved.
- **Transit** = public bus, BRT, light rail or commuter rail on regular or augmented public service.
- **Event shuttle (non-car)** = charter coaches, bar buses and similar where the rider did not drive to the boarding point.
- **Rides vs. people:** Metro Transit counts each round-tripping attendee as **two rides** ([Star Tribune excerpt, 2014](https://m.startribune.com/a-quarter-of-vikings-fans-take-green-line-to-the-game/278048221/)). This is confirmed by the agency's own State Fair arithmetic: 654,000 rides equalled "just over 16 percent" of 2017 attendance, which only works if rides are divided by two ([Metro Transit](https://www.metrotransittest.org/state-fair-green-line-see-record-ridership)).
- **Regular-transit park-and-ride** (driving to an LRT or express-bus lot): car-involved in principle, but access mode is unmeasured for game-day light rail riders. Primary values below treat game-day LRT riders as non-car; the **upper end of each range** covers the case where a large minority drove to the line. Dedicated event park-and-ride (State Fair free lots, express-bus lots, Renaissance Festival and amphitheater shuttles, Allianz remote lot) is always counted as car-involved.

## 1. Summary answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Recommended existing city type | **`hub`** |
| Is `transit` (0.40) supported anywhere? | No. The most transit-oriented venue (Allianz Field) still shows roughly 0.55 car-involved; no measured or projected venue reaches 0.40. |
| Is `sprawl` defensible? | It is the strongest alternative. The largest crowds in the region (State Fair, Shakopee venues) are 0.93–0.98 car-involved. But the region also has dense, overlapping downtown/campus event clusters on shared rail lines and freeway approaches, which fits `hub` better. |
| Core downtown/campus stadium car share | About **0.70** (capacity-weighted, estimated) |
| All estimated 5,000+ fixed venues | About **0.73** capacity-weighted (0.74 if the unmeasured arena is filled with the 0.85 fallback) |
| Biggest event crowd | Minnesota State Fair: about **0.93** car-involved (2023 counts), despite the fair's own headline that ~40% arrive by "mass transit" — most of that is drive-to-shuttle |

## 2. Regional background (commuting data — background only, not event travel)

Commuting and household-survey data describe ordinary travel, not event travel, and are **not** used to set any value below.

- The Metropolitan Council notes that work commuting is only about 30–40% of all trips and that commute transit share (its illustrative example is about 3%) is not the same as all-purpose mode share ([Met Council Transportation Committee, Aug 2024](https://metrocouncil.org/Council-Meetings/Committees/Transportation-Committee/2024/08-26-2024/Info-2-_-Ridership.aspx)).
- The 2000 Travel Behavior Inventory found transit carried 2.3% of all person trips in the seven-county area and auto drivers about two-thirds ([Met Council TBI 2000 brief](https://metrocouncil.org/Transportation/Publications-And-Resources/Planning/Travel-Behavior-Inventory/TBI2000TBIBrief1-pdf.aspx)). This is dated; the 2023 TBI exists ([NREL data record](https://www.nrel.gov/transportation/secure-transportation-data/tsdc-cleansed-data/2023-twin-cities-travel-behavior-inventory-household-travel-survey)) but its published mode-share tables were not retrieved in this pass.

**Takeaway:** everyday travel is overwhelmingly car-based. Event travel to the downtown and campus stadiums is markedly less car-dependent than that baseline, which is the main reason this region is not `sprawl` at its core.

**Historical context:** at the Metrodome about 10% (≈6,700) of Vikings fans took light rail; at TCF Bank Stadium in 2014 it was 24% on the Green Line, counted directly as 12,780 and 12,737 fans at two games ([Star Tribune excerpt 2014](https://m.startribune.com/pedestrian-bridge-in-the-works-for-vikes-stadium/272734301/); [Star Tribune excerpt 2014b](https://m.startribune.com/a-quarter-of-vikings-fans-take-green-line-to-the-game/278048221/)).

**Ridership inference check (Monday Night Football, 3 Oct 2016)** — shown to demonstrate the method, not to replace the agency's figure:

| Step | Value | Source / note |
|---|---|---|
| Green Line rides on game day (Monday) | 66,018 | [Metro Transit](https://www.metrotransit.org/monday-night-football-helps-set-new-single-day-light-rail-ridership-record) |
| Baseline: Sept 2016 average weekday Green Line rides | 46,109 | same source |
| Increment | 19,909 rides | subtraction |
| Divide by 2 (round trips) | ≈ 9,950 people | agency convention |
| Attendance denominator | ~66,000 | stadium football capacity |
| Implied Green Line share | ≈ 15% | |
| Blue Line increment | Not computable — no Blue Line baseline published in the same source | gap |
| Biases | Overstates: transfers between lines count twice; non-fan event-day riders. Understates: fans who rode only one way. | |

Green Line alone yields ~15%; Metro Transit's "around a quarter" overall implies roughly 10 points from the Blue Line. The two are consistent.

### Do a few huge outlying events obscure the downtown pattern?

Yes.

- The State Fair alone moves the crowd-weighted figure from 0.69 to 0.82.
- The Shakopee venues (MSP-08, MSP-10) sit at 0.97–0.98.
- The fair is not geographically outlying: Falcon Heights lies between the two downtowns. It behaves like a suburban destination because its transit is built on drive-to-shuttle lots.
- The downtown and campus stadiums, taken alone, cluster around 0.55–0.75.

### Recommendation: `hub`

**Rationale:**

- **Distinct, close-spaced clusters.** The region has separate event clusters in downtown Minneapolis (west and east), the UMN East Bank, the Midway, the fairgrounds and downtown Saint Paul.
- **Shared rail spine.** The clusters are strung along one light rail spine (Green Line, plus the Blue Line into downtown Minneapolis) and a few freeway approaches (I-94, I-35W, I-394, I-35E).
- **Documented same-day overlaps.** There are repeated same-day multi-venue peaks and explicit sharing of parking assets (UMN lots and State Fair lots trade roles with Gophers and Allianz events).
- **Downtown shares are well below a driving region.** Measured transit at the core stadiums (20–31%) puts car-involved shares near 0.55–0.75, which is unlike a pure driving region.

**Strongest contrary evidence (for `sprawl`):**

1. The single largest crowd, the State Fair at up to ~240,000 people a day, is about 0.93 car-involved once drive-to-shuttle is counted.
2. The newest large venue (Mystic Lake Amphitheater, 19,000) and the Renaissance Festival are ~0.97–0.98.
3. All measured transit shares predate 2020, and Northstar game service has since ended. Present-day downtown shares may be higher than shown.

**Evidence against `transit`:** no venue reaches 0.40 even on its most transit-favourable projection (Allianz projected 43.5% before LRT drive-access; observed ~0.55).

**Venue exceptions:**

- MSP-06 (Allianz) is the most transit-oriented.
- MSP-08, MSP-09 and MSP-10 are sprawl-like and should keep venue-specific overrides rather than relying on the city type.

**Clusters, shared roads and shared audiences (reported separately, per the brief):**

- *Clusters:* Minneapolis downtown-west (MSP-03/04); Minneapolis downtown-east (MSP-01/02, Armory); UMN East Bank (MSP-05, Williams, Mariucci); Midway (MSP-06); fairgrounds (MSP-09); downtown Saint Paul (MSP-07, Roy Wilkins, CHS Field); Shakopee (MSP-08, MSP-10, Canterbury).
- *Shared roads and assets:* I-94 (ties downtown Minneapolis, the UMN area, the Midway and downtown Saint Paul); Snelling Avenue (Midway and fairgrounds); Southbridge and Eagle Creek park-and-rides (Renaissance Festival and amphitheater shuttles); UMN lots (Fair and Gophers); fairgrounds lots (Fair and Allianz).
- *Shared audiences:* the Vikings/Twins/Gophers fan base overlaps; State Fair and Gophers season openers coincide in late August; summer concerts are now split between downtown and Shakopee.

No new distance rule or hand-access flag is proposed.

## 9. Boundary recommendation and inconsistencies between briefs

**Recommendation:** use the Metropolitan Council's **seven-county region** (Anoka, Carver, Dakota, Hennepin, Ramsey, Scott, Washington), with Minneapolis and Saint Paul treated as one connected pair.

**Edge municipalities** (inside the boundary but behaving as car-dominant edge clusters):

- Shakopee and Prior Lake (Scott County): MSP-08, MSP-10, Canterbury.
- Blaine (Anoka): National Sports Center; also a State Fair express-bus hub.
- Maple Grove, Cottage Grove, Burnsville, Eagan, Chaska, Carver and Eden Prairie: these act as event park-and-ride origins rather than venues.

**Outside the boundary (audience origins only):** Sherburne County (Big Lake, Elk River; former Northstar), and Mankato, Rochester, St. Cloud and Duluth, which the Allianz TMP names as charter-bus origins.

**Basis:**

- Event-travel catchments: State Fair and Renaissance Festival express and park-and-ride lots all fall within the seven counties ([State Fair plan](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2024/mandated/241145.pdf); [MVTA](https://www.mvta.com/news-items/mvta-achieves-record-breaking-renaissance-festival-ridership/)).
- Season-ticket-holder origin mapping was used in the Allianz TMP, though the map itself is not reproduced in text.

**Gap:** sourced county population and commuting-flow figures were **not retrieved** in this pass. The boundary therefore rests on event-travel evidence, not on population or commuting thresholds. Re-check against Met Council population estimates before locking.

**Inconsistencies between the brief and current facts:**

1. "Xcel Energy Center" is now **Grand Casino Arena**; the rename took effect 3 Sept 2025 ([KEYC/AP](https://www.keyc.com/2025/06/30/wild-now-play-grand-casino-arena-after-new-naming-rights-deal-succeeds-xcel-energy-center/)).
2. The starting leads omit **Mystic Lake Amphitheater** (19,000, opened June 2026). This is the largest new venue in the region and sits at the suburban edge.
3. The brief's companion venue answer was unavailable, so row IDs here are provisional keys.
4. "University of Minnesota venues" is unspecific. Only Huntington Bank Stadium has evidence; Williams Arena and Mariucci are listed as gaps.
5. "Connected pair" (starting search area) is narrower than the actual event catchment, which spans all seven counties.

## 10. Data gaps, model limitations, source conflicts and access notes

**Data gaps**

- No post-2020 measured transit share for any downtown stadium.
- No measured split at all for Target Center, Grand Casino Arena, Mystic Lake, theatres or open-site festivals.
- State Fair 2024–2026 free park-and-ride totals were not retrieved.

**Model limitations**

- The `hub` and `sprawl` fallbacks share the same 0.85 value. The choice between them therefore matters mainly for spill between nearby crowds, not for fallback car share.
- For unmeasured downtown venues the 0.85 fallback is probably **high** relative to measured downtown neighbours (~0.70). This is noted, not tuned.
- Multi-day totals (State Fair, Renaissance Festival, Pride) are converted to per-day crowds only where daily data exist.

**Source conflicts preserved**

- Twins transit share: 20% vs. 23% vs. 25%.
- Allianz opener: 28% vs. ~33%; season 31%.
- MVTA 2023 State Fair: 113,000 vs. "over 110,000".
- Pride expected attendance: 400,000+ (2026) vs. 600,000 (2024).
- State Fair "mass transit ~40%" vs. this answer's ~1.4% non-car transit. This is a definitional difference, not a factual one.

**Access notes**

- Star Tribune articles were read only as publicly shown excerpts. Full texts may sit behind a subscription and were **not** bypassed.
- The Allianz Field TMP and the State Fair transportation plan were fully readable PDFs.
