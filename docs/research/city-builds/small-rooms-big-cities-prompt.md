# Research brief: the 1,000–5,000-seat rooms in the biggest US cities

Status: open. Written Oct 16, 2026. For any research model that can browse.

## What this is for
Fan/Friction is a personal log of live events (sports and concerts), with a "friction" read for each night in a city. The app lists events from about 1,000 people up; only events of 5,000+ feed the read, and smaller rooms show muted (Kylie, Oct 6 and Oct 9, 2026). The first venue research for each city looked only at rooms of 5,000+, so the big cities are missing most of their concert clubs and theaters. A night at the Beacon Theatre or the Hollywood Palladium can't be logged against a room the app knows, and those rooms never appear on the map.

## Cities, in this order
New York, Los Angeles, Chicago, Dallas–Fort Worth, Houston, Washington DC, Philadelphia, Atlanta, Miami, Phoenix. Then, if there is time, Boston and the San Francisco Bay Area. Use each metro area as a whole (New York includes North Jersey and Long Island; Los Angeles includes Orange County and Ventura County).

## What counts
Every **room that regularly hosts ticketed public events for about 1,000 to 5,000 people**: concert halls and theaters (including touring Broadway houses, when they also host concerts or comedy), music clubs and ballrooms, small amphitheaters, college arenas and fieldhouses, minor-league ballparks and stadiums of that size, racetrack grandstands, and convention halls that host ticketed shows. A room of 5,000+ that the app is missing also goes in (say so).

Leave out: rooms under about 1,000; restaurants and bars with stages; private event spaces; churches, except when they run a regular public concert series; buildings closed before 2016, except as history inside a current row.

**Rooms the app already has** are listed at the end. Don't repeat them; correct one only if a figure there is wrong (say so in a separate section).

**Rooms the app's ticket feed already sees but can't place** (from its listings, Oct 15, 2026): Boston: Royale, Boch Center Wang Theatre. Philadelphia: Academy of Music, TD Pavilion at the Mann. Miami: the Broward Center's Au-Rene and Amaturo theaters and Abdo New River Room, the Arsht Center's Ziff Ballet Opera House and Knight Concert Hall. Houston: White Oak Music Hall (and its lawn). Dallas–Fort Worth: Billy Bob's Texas. Bay Area: Frost Amphitheatre. Include these.

## For each room
| Field | What I need |
|---|---|
| Name today | Official name in 2026, and the name Ticketmaster lists it under if different |
| Earlier names | Every name since 2016, each with the date it changed |
| Area | Neighborhood, or town and county |
| Coordinates | Decimal latitude and longitude of the building's main entrance |
| Capacity by setup | Seated and standing figures for each setup the room actually uses (concert end-stage, general-admission floor, theater, basketball…). If a figure changed since 2016, old and new with the year. |
| Roof | Open-air, covered, or indoor |
| Campus | Whether it shares a building, campus or block with a bigger venue (a theater inside an arena building, a club next to a stadium), and which |
| Kind | Concert hall, theater, club or ballroom, amphitheater, college arena, ballpark, other |
| Ticketmaster venue id | When you can find it on the room's Ticketmaster page (the id in the URL), else blank |
| Source for each capacity | A link. Prefer the room's own site (production or rental pages), then the operator, then a reliable report. |

## Rules
- **Label every number:** official, reported, or estimated. Never a bare number.
- Where sources disagree, give both and say which you'd use and why. Standing (general admission) and seated figures are different numbers; give both when both exist.
- Say which rooms you **could not confirm** rather than guessing. If a page won't load, list it; don't work around it.
- No costs, sign-ups or contacting anyone. Public sources only.
