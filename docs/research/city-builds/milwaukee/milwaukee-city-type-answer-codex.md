# Milwaukee city type and event travel evidence

**Research cut-off: October 11, 2026. Recommendation: driving (`sprawl`), provisionally.** Confidence is moderate in the qualitative regional classification and low in any numerical regional car share. The public evidence reviewed does **not** establish that 85% of Milwaukee event attendees arrive by car. It also does not establish a transit-dominant region. Keep the existing driving fallback of **0.85** provisionally wherever a defensible venue override is unavailable; do not present it as measured Milwaukee behavior. The existing `hub` fallback is also 0.85, so changing between those settings would affect the model’s other existing behavior, not resolve the missing travel measurement.

This answer concerns pressure from overlapping event crowds, not navigation or live traffic. Capacities identify possible crowd sizes; parking and transit schedules identify travel options and constraints. Neither establishes how attendees actually traveled. No new constants, distance rules, access flags or personal-rating adjustments are proposed.

## Scope, dates and how to read the tables

Use **Milwaukee, Waukesha, Ozaukee and Washington counties** as the core research boundary. HUD’s January 1, 2023 Milwaukee–Waukesha housing-market analysis identifies those four counties, and Wisconsin’s March 2024 metropolitan outlook uses the same four-county MSA. [HUD analysis](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/publications/pdf/MilwaukeeWaukeshaWI-CHMA-23.pdf); [Wisconsin metropolitan outlook](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/DORReports/2024-03-wi-msa-outlook.pdf). This is an administratively reproducible boundary, not evidence that every pair of events within it shares an audience or access road.

The companion chat, **“Research Milwaukee venue table,”** was inspected while its report was still being prepared. The inventory below was therefore researched independently and is not claimed to be a complete reconciliation with an unpublished final venue answer. Exact building/room names, municipalities and setups are retained to make that reconciliation possible. The coverage register explicitly includes unresolved rooms, school facilities and edge cases rather than silently dropping them.

Unless a publication date is given, a linked venue page is a current public page checked for this answer on **October 11, 2026**. An older dated capacity is identified as such. Service described for a past festival applies to that edition, not automatically to its next occurrence. “Unknown” means no defensible public person-mode fraction was established in this review; it does not mean zero transit or zero walking.

| Notation | Meaning and rule |
|---|---|
| C | Fraction of attendee **persons** whose journey includes private car, carpool, taxi or rideshare, including a ride to a remote parking lot. Vehicle counts are not person counts. |
| PT | Public bus/rail share among people with no car leg under this definition. A car-to-transit park-and-ride traveler belongs in C, with PT recorded as an additional journey leg if available. A traveler walking/biking to transit belongs in PT. |
| ES | Dedicated event-shuttle share among people with no car leg. A car-to-remote-lot-to-shuttle traveler belongs in C; shuttle use remains a useful separate operational count. Hotel-shuttle passengers are not automatically car arrivals at the event, but their earlier journey and the survey’s origin definition must be stated. |
| WBO | Walk, bicycle, scooter and other non-car journeys not assigned to PT or ES. Walking from a garage is C, not WBO. |
| U / U / U / U | C, PT, ES and WBO all unknown. There is no measured split to total; **sum-to-100% cannot be tested**. Missing categories must not be set to zero. |
| U0 | Implementation recommendation: **unknown override**, no defensible point estimate or empirical range, low quantitative confidence; existing `sprawl` fallback **0.85 applies provisionally**. This is an explicit missing-data decision, not an estimated venue share. |
| Operations profile | The named profile in the next table supplies parking, public transit, special service, start/finish limits and sourced approaches for each matching venue row. A profile shares documented options, not a mode percentage. |

For a future observed survey, mutually exclusive person categories should sum to 100% under one consistent journey definition. Preserve mixed-mode leg counts separately. Rideshare includes both pickup and drop-off pressure, but its vehicle movements cannot be converted into attendees without occupancy and empty-vehicle information. For a downtown attendee already staying nearby, state whether the surveyed journey begins at lodging or at home; those are different questions.

### Ridership inference, including the arithmetic and its limits

A usable inference would require **event-attributable passenger boardings minus comparable non-event baseline boardings**, removal of staff/non-attendee travel, a verified average number of counted legs per attendee visit, and an attendance denominator for the same dates and service area. Then classify car-to-lot and non-car users. Baseline subtraction alone cannot distinguish them. FHWA’s event-planning guidance supports collecting patron mode and origin information rather than treating generic transport counts as a complete split. [FHWA planned-special-event guidance](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwaop04010/chapter5_03.htm).

| Diagnostic calculation | Explicit assumption | Result and why it is not an implementation value |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 Summerfest dedicated shuttle: 45,000 ÷ 2 ÷ 624,407 | All reported rides are attendee boardings; every attendee takes exactly two counted legs; no employees or repeat/one-way anomalies. Baseline set to zero **only for this dedicated service**, not CONNECT 1. | **3.60%** shuttle-assisted visit equivalents, calculated here from the linked figures above. This is neither a unique-person estimate nor a non-car fraction; car-to-lot use is unknown. No empirical uncertainty interval can be supplied. |
| 2018 State Fair: 43,500 ÷ 1,037,982 | “Fairgoers” means attendee visits, counted once. | **4.19%**, calculated here; historical and mode-incomplete. |
| 2018 State Fair: 43,500 ÷ 2 ÷ 1,037,982 | The same numerator instead means boardings and every attendee has two counted legs. | **2.10%**, calculated here. The two results show unit ambiguity, **not** a statistical range or current share. |
| 2023 CONNECT 1 | Would need matched weekday/weekend non-festival stop/direction counts and staff/destination adjustments. | Baseline unavailable. No net event numerator, return correction or valid share calculated. |

The old Summerfest bus case and the Brewers poll are meaningful contrary evidence to an unqualified claim that Milwaukee crowds are uniformly 85% car. They are insufficient to supply today’s compatible car-person value.

## Regional weighting: reproducible calculations, not invented modal averages

For either weighting method, the desired car-person share is **Σ(weight × compatible venue car-person share) ÷ Σ(weight)**. A missing car share cannot be replaced with zero. Computing only sites with transit statistics would introduce selection bias. Here **none of the reference basket’s sites has a usable current complete person-mode split**, so the observed regional result is **unknown** under both methods.

## 1. Recommended city type, reasons and strongest contrary evidence

**Recommend `sprawl` provisionally for the four-county region.** The large ballpark, fair/race complex and outer venues document extensive motor access or on-site parking arrangements; the transit network has important evening/weekend limits, and dedicated service is seasonal and event-specific. Those are relevant to plausible regional crowd access pressure. Confidence in the classification is moderate; it is an inference from infrastructure and service evidence, **not** a measured attendee-majority finding or proof of the 85% fallback.

**Strongest contrary evidence:** downtown venues share walkable lodging, buses and streetcar access; Summerfest has documented substantial historical bus/shuttle use and current dedicated service; the 2026 Brewers preference poll strongly features bar shuttles. These support a possible `hub` pattern for downtown and some festival/game occasions. Mixed-mode car legs and the lack of representative observed surveys prevent those sources from establishing a regional transit majority or a defensible lower car override. A downtown-specific measured split could change the conclusion. Do not describe all venues as 85% car just because the regional type is driving.

## 4. Important limitations, missing coverage and source conflicts

- **No complete compatible person split:** every current venue override is unknown. This review’s failure to locate a usable public survey does not prove that organizers possess none. Private operator data are outside scope.
- **Person/vehicle/journey-unit mismatch:** occupancy assumptions, boardings, poll responses, parking stalls, ticket purchases and multi-day entries cannot be substituted for unique attendee journeys. Drive-to-bar and drive-to-park-and-ride can make a bus-heavy count simultaneously car-assisted.
- **Evidence age and transfer:** Brooklyn arena survey is not Milwaukee; 2000 Summerfest and 2018 State Fair service do not describe 2026; former Bradley Center counts do not become Fiserv observations. No imported comparison supports a numerical estimate here.
- **Inventory gaps:** unpublished companion final keys, schools/gyms, several campus/community rooms, remaining lakefront-stage limits, Expo layouts, fair grandstands and edge venues remain unresolved. The explicit registers identify them; the partial baskets must not be advertised as complete regional coverage.
- **Capacity conflicts:** Potawatomi Amplified’s dated 2024 outdoor limits; current ballpark total after 2025 changes; Eagles Ballroom/operator versus installation report; Varsity lecture/concert and component sums; current MSOE versus design seating; older Shattuck/Symphony figures; Main Stage versus Mile layouts. Use specific manifests when available. Small Coliseum component discrepancy is reconciled to the host’s advertised total; material conflicts remain visible.
- **Service gaps:** route-wide first/last envelopes are not direction-specific venue-stop guarantees; transfers, accessible service, last shuttle capacity, weather, extra innings/overtime and concert encores are unmeasured. A late-night route exists without guaranteeing every destination. Conversely, an unverified timetable is not zero transit availability.
- **Access/source gaps:** full CSL Brewers PDF and MSO’s [2024–25 annual report](https://www.mso.org/app/uploads/2025/12/2425_MSO_AnnualReport-1.pdf) exceeded normal tool size limits; the district redevelopment PDF and an arena parking attachment were not fully fetchable. No bypass was attempted. Indexed findings are explicitly weaker than a verified full table. Washington fair pages mix edition dates; ridership components in the 2023 Summerfest report do not reconcile.
- **Crowd overlap:** sharing a garage/road does not prove shared attendees. General fair/festival gate totals can include headliner patrons, employees and repeat visits. Peak simultaneous attendance, daily splits, event finish pulses and arrival distributions are missing. A capacity-weighted type is not a live gridlock prediction.
- **Temporal and cohort coverage:** sports season means omit concerts, winter/summer differences, special games and fairs. The Milwaukee men’s cohort combines two home sites; separate building-specific means remain missing. No defensible regional confidence interval is available; uncertainty is missing-data uncertainty, not a fabricated percentage band.
