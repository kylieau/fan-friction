# Columbus, Ohio — Event-Travel Evidence and City-Type Assessment for Fan/Friction Gridlock

**Research date:** Sunday, October 11, 2026 (all sources retrieved this date; event dates noted per row)
**Scope:** Columbus and the connected central Ohio event area (start: Franklin County and nearby towns)
**Purpose:** Assess the existing city settings (`sprawl`, `hub`, `transit`) against public evidence of how people actually reach Columbus event venues. This is not navigation, live traffic or trip planning.

---

## 0. Read this first: what the evidence can and cannot support

- **No measured event mode split was found for any Columbus venue or event.** I searched venue/team pages, Ohio State University (OSU) transportation pages, COTA (Central Ohio Transit Authority) releases, City of Columbus traffic material, and planning documents for the Crew stadium. None publishes an observed person-mode split for attendees. Every car-share value below is therefore an **estimate (this report)** built from public inputs, with ranges and stated assumptions. There are **zero measured** and **zero projected** values.
- **The companion venue answer was not available in this conversation.** Venues were enumerated independently; row IDs (`CMH-V…` for fixed venues, `CMH-E…` for event crowds) are this report's own and will need mapping to the companion answer's rows.
- **Ordinary commute data appears only as background** (Section 6) and is never used as an event split.
- Park-and-ride and remote-lot shuttles are counted as **car-involved** throughout (see Section 3).

## 3. Method

**Treatment of park-and-ride and remote shuttles.** Any attendee who drove to a remote lot (OSU West Campus, Columbus State, Metro Place, Columbus Zoo, COTA park-and-ride) is counted in "Car", because the car trip loads the road network on the way to the lot. Only shuttle riders who boarded without driving (for example from a hotel) are counted as non-car shuttle. COTA fixed-route riders who drove to a park-and-ride are likewise car-involved; the share of COTA event riders who did so is unpublished, which is a key uncertainty for E03.

**Event transit vs regular service.** For every row except Red, White & BOOM! in earlier years, the only public transit is COTA regular fixed-route service. No dedicated event bus service was found for any venue in 2026.

**Ohio Stadium (CMH-V01) estimate, shown in full.**
1. Denominator: capacity 102,780 ([OSU Athletics](https://ohiostatebuckeyes.com/sports/2023/6/2/ohio-stadium)) as a proxy for typical football attendance (season average not retrieved).
2. Students: OSU sold 29,614 student packages against a 28,333-seat allotment in 2014 ([Eleven Warriors](https://www.elevenwarriors.com/ohio-state-football/2014/06/36802/while-student-attendance-declines-ohio-state-records-1st-sellout-in-3-years-mulls-wifi)); OSU said 30,000 in 2010 ([OSU News](https://news.osu.edu/ohio-state-2010-football-season/)). Assumed ~28,000 student attendees (current figure is a gap).
3. Assumed 70–90% of students arrive on foot/bike/scooter (campus and near-campus housing; a dedicated scooter zone exists, [OSU concert page](https://ohiostatebuckeyes.com/sports/2026/4/6/concert-parking)), so 10–30% car-involved.
4. Assumed 85–95% of the ~75,000 non-students arrive by car (including shuttle-from-lot and RV), with COTA and charter buses making up most of the remainder.
5. Result: (75,000 × 0.85–0.95 + 28,000 × 0.10–0.30) ÷ 103,000 ≈ **0.65–0.77**, central ≈ 0.71–0.72; widened to 0.62–0.82 for denominator and ticket-buyer/attendee uncertainty.

**Arena District estimates (V03, V04, V06, V07).** Inputs: 18,000+ district spaces ([Crew](https://www.columbuscrew.com/news/columbus-crew-supporters-encouraged-to-arrive-early-for-home-opening-match-this-)), no event fares ([COTA](https://www.cota.com/partner-resource-hub)), bus-only transit. Non-car share is assumed to come mostly from walking by downtown/Short North/Victorian Village residents and hotel guests. Upper non-car bound informed by comparison: even a light-rail-served soccer stadium in St. Paul projected only 13% walk/bike and 1% local bus at capacity ([St. Paul Allianz Field TMP, 2019 projection](https://www.stpaul.gov/sites/default/files/Media%20Root/Planning%20&%20Economic%20Development/Allianz-Field-TMP-Full.pdf)); transit-rich Philadelphia peer arenas averaged 37% auto plus 7% ride-hail ([City of Philadelphia TIA, 2024](https://www.phila.gov/media/20240731112508/Traffic-Impact-Analysis-FINAL_ENG_7.2024.pdf)) — a transit-rich bound Columbus does not resemble. These comparisons bound the estimate; they are not Columbus evidence.

**No parking-space-to-mode-share conversion was performed.** Parking counts are reported as supply context only; occupancy and vehicle occupancy data are absent.

| 2025 | Dedicated express from 4 park-and-rides dropped | [COTA, 2025](https://cota.com/blog/cota-ready-to-transport-thousands-of-customers-during-annual-red-white-and-boom-celebration-july-3/) |
| 2026 | Regular fixed-route service only, no dedicated express | [COTA](https://cota.com/boom/) |

**Inference (this report):**
- *Baseline subtraction:* the ~2019 figure is already incremental ("additional"). The other figures are not stated as incremental; COTA's systemwide weekday ridership of ~39,000 in Q4 2025 ([Wikipedia, citing COTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Ohio_Transit_Authority)) shows the scale of ordinary service, but the downtown-evening baseline that should be subtracted is unpublished.
- *Denominator:* 400,000 expected attendees (2026 media estimate), applied with low confidence to all years.
- *Return trips:* if "riders" counts persons, 15,000–20,000 ÷ 400,000 = **3.75–5.0%**; if it counts boardings (two per round-trip person), 7,500–10,000 persons = **1.9–2.5%**.
- *Car involvement:* an unknown portion of these riders drove to park-and-rides and so are car-involved.
- *Conclusion:* non-car COTA share for Boom is roughly **2–5%**, and has fallen since 2009 as dedicated express ended. The 4% "Bus" allocation in Section 2a sits at the top of that range. Raw boardings were not converted directly into a mode share without these stated assumptions.

## End-of-report answers

### 1. Recommended existing city type and rationale; strongest contrary evidence

**Recommendation: `sprawl`.** Every Columbus venue estimate falls between 0.72 and 0.93 car share; the region has no rail; COTA runs no event fare programs and has cut dedicated express service for its largest event; and several of the largest crowds (State Fair, Sonic Temple, Dublin events, Little Brown Jug) are at car-access sites ringed by surface parking. The `transit` default of 0.4 is clearly unsupported for any row.

**Strongest contrary evidence (toward `hub`):** the Arena District is a compact, walkable cluster where five venues share 18,000+ garage spaces and approaches and where same-evening overlaps are documented; and Ohio Stadium's large student walk-in share (estimated 0.72 car) is unlike a pure driving venue. Because `sprawl` and `hub` share the same 0.85 fallback car share, this choice changes only spill behavior between nearby crowds. If, in the app's logic, `hub` mainly means "dense shared-approach cluster with more spill", the Arena District evidence would argue for `hub`; I did not have the app's spill definition, so this is flagged as the main judgment call.

**Venue exceptions:** CMH-V01 (OSU football, ~0.72) is the one row where a venue-specific override materially departs from the 0.85 fallback.
