# New York: arrival shares by venue, three weightings, and the city sentence

Answer to `new-york-arrival-shares` brief. Written Oct 14, 2026 by Claude (Opus 5.5). Reconciles with `new-york-city-type-answer.md` (Oct 6) and `city-type-plain-words-answer.md` (Oct 14).

Labels used throughout: **measured** = counted (turnstiles, tickets, surveys with counts); **announced** = a venue, team or agency statement; **reported** = a figure in a document or article that cites someone else's data; **estimated** = my number or a number with no data behind it. Every number I computed is shown with its arithmetic in the self-audit.

## The short version

1. **One stored figure is clearly wrong: Madison Square Garden.** MSG's own surveys, cited by the City Planning Commission in July 2023, put private cars at 23–38% of arrivals and taxis/black cars at another 5–9%; transit is 50–61%. The stored 20% is too low. Recommended: **37%** (range 28–47%), counting taxis and ride-hail as "by car" because they use the same streets and curbs.
2. **Nothing newer exists** for Barclays, Yankee Stadium, Citi Field, Prudential Center, Radio City or Forest Hills. Keep those figures. Citi Field should be flagged: its lots are partly closed for casino construction from early 2026 to about 2030.
3. **The three weightings disagree**, as the Oct 6 and Oct 14 answers suspected: seat-weighted **56%** by car, event-weighted **42%**, attendance-weighted **52%** (ten venues; ranges in section 2).
4. **Neither stock sentence is true.** Counting people (attendance), New York is about half and half. Counting nights out (events), most crowds come by transit. Recommended one-off sentence: **"New York: most events are a train ride; stadium crowds often drive."** (67 characters).
5. **Type:** switch to **transit**, but only together with per-venue spillover. If per-venue spillover isn't built, keep **hub**, because the clusters where spillover is a road problem (Meadowlands, Queens, Long Island) are car-heavy and 15% fits them better than 10%.

## 1. Newer figures for the ten venues

### Madison Square Garden — change 20% → 37%

- **Newest figure:** In the July 12, 2023 City Planning Commission report on MSG's special permit, the applicant's surveys show commuter rail at 22–37% of trips, subway 18–35%, bus 1–4%, private vehicles 23–38% and taxis/black cars 5–9%. Combined transit is 50–61%. Label: **reported, 2023** (the report does not give survey dates, sample sizes or method). [CPC report C 230238 ZSM](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/about/cpc/230238.pdf)
- **Corroboration (weak):** In 2005, Brooklyn's borough president said about 40% of Garden audiences arrive by car. Label: **announced, 2005**. [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2005/12/06/brooklyn-arena-traffic-could-mimic-garden-s)
- **Useful for gridlock:** the same CPC report says MSG's data showed no significant difference in traffic volumes between event and non-event days, and the arena has no parking of its own (about 4,300 garage spaces and 1,000–1,500 evening curb spaces within a quarter mile).
- **Event mix:** 2017–2019 averaged about 235 arena events a year: 113 sports (48%), 89 concerts (38%), 33 other (14%). No mode split by event type was published, so Knicks/Rangers vs. concerts stays blank.
- **Recommendation:** 37% = midpoint of private vehicles (30.5%) plus midpoint of taxis (7%), rounded. If Fan/Friction counts only cars that park, use 30%. Congestion pricing (2025) probably pushes this down a little; there is no venue figure to show by how much, so I have not adjusted for it.
- **The Theater at MSG** has no figure of its own; it shares the building and entrances. Apply 37% and mark "check".

### Barclays Center — keep 25%

- Nothing newer than the stored 2013 survey. A 2012 pre-opening projection presented by Sam Schwartz expected about 40% of attendees on the subway and about 30% driving at first. Label: **estimated (projection), 2012**. [Second Avenue Sagas](https://secondavenuesagas.com/2012/05/23/a-brooklyn-arena-rises-and-so-do-transportation-worries/)
- The arena still has no garage or lots of its own; nothing since 2013 would raise the car share.

### Radio City Music Hall — keep 15%, mark "check"

- No measured or reported figure found. The MSG finding is a warning: Midtown car shares, once taxis are counted, may run higher than the transit tier assumed. Radio City's biggest product, the Christmas Spectacular, draws families and tour groups, some by coach. I have not changed the number because there is no data.

### Yankee Stadium — keep 50%

- Nothing newer than the stored figures. The WNYC piece behind them (MTA data, 2011) says about 45% take transit once Metro-North, buses and ferries are added to the subway. Label: **reported, 2011**. [WNYC](https://wnyc.org/story/283116-more-fans-look-to-take-transit-to-see-yankees-than-mets/)
- **Garages:** the company that runs the 9,000-space stadium parking system has not met its bond payments since 2011 and owed the city about $162 million by late 2022. That is consistent with fewer drivers than planned, but it is not a count. [THE CITY, 2022](https://thecity.nyc/2022/12/21/23519527/yankee-stadium-parking-payments-162m-owed-city)
- **Grand Central Madison (2023)** gives Long Island fans a shorter rail trip to the stadium, per the MTA. No ridership effect has been published. [MTA](https://www.mta.info/press-release/photos-hundreds-of-fans-take-nostalgia-train-ride-2024-yankees-home-opener)
- Weekday vs. weekend splits: none found.

### Citi Field — keep 60%, mark "check" for 2026–2030

- Nothing newer than 2011 MTA data (25–30% by subway for an average game, 30–35% for Subway Series games, LIRR not counted). Label: **reported, 2011**. [WNYC](https://wnyc.org/story/283116-more-fans-look-to-take-transit-to-see-yankees-than-mets/)
- **Casino build:** Metropolitan Park won a state license in December 2025, and prep work began clearing the Citi Field lots in early 2026; completion is targeted for 2030. [Yogonet](https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2026/02/26/117781-groundwork-underway-for-metropolitan-park-casino-and-entertainment-complex-in-queens) The Mets told fans parking would be more limited, drive-up spots might not be available, and added game-day shuttle buses from five malls. [Queens Chronicle](https://www.qchron.com/editions/queenswide/mets-offering-fans-alternatives-to-cars/article_7123a1d9-b6b6-5579-8843-962f46ff9d90.html) Construction is running about five and a half months behind its filed schedule. [Queens Daily Eagle](https://queenseagle.com/all/2026/5/15/metropolitan-park-casino-construction-running-months-behind-schedule)
- **Long run:** the project promises more parking than today, about 6,000 spaces above the current 7,423. [Hudson River Blue](https://www.hudsonriverblue.com/metropolitan-park-citi-field-willets-point-casino-community-approval-faqs-answered/) So the car share may dip during construction and recover after. No survey shows either.

### US Open — keep 37%

- The National Tennis Center's COO said in August 2024 that more than 60% of fans take mass transit. Label: **announced, 2024**. [ABC7](https://abc7ny.com/post/mta-usta-kickoff-us-open-fan-week-announcing-added-subway-long-island-rail-road-service/15194434/)
- Same lots as Citi Field: on Mets home dates, US Open drivers are sent elsewhere ([TickPick guide, 2026](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/how-to-get-to-the-us-open/)), and the USTA has disputed whether its fans keep parking priority under the casino lease ([Yogonet](https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2026/02/26/117781-groundwork-underway-for-metropolitan-park-casino-and-entertainment-complex-in-queens)).

### Prudential Center — keep 45%

- Nothing newer than 2007–08: in the last two months of that season, 53% of hockey fans used mass transit. Label: **reported, 2008** (hockey only). [SportsBusiness Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessdaily.com/Journal/Issues/2008/05/25/Sports-Facility-Of-The-Year/Prudential-Center.aspx) No concert figure found.

### MetLife Stadium — keep 85% (or 80% blended), mark "check"

- **NFL:** stored ~87% (NJ Transit, 2010); nothing newer found.
- **World Cup 2026 (car-restricted event):** NJ Transit planned for up to 40,000 rail riders per match but sold 22,000–26,000. Label: **announced, 2026**. [Gothamist](https://gothamist.com/news/world-cup-marks-a-rare-goaaaaaal-for-new-york-area-transit) The stadium welcomed more than 645,000 fans across eight matches ([Pollstar/VenuesNow](https://news.pollstar.com/?p=1226392)), about 80,600 per match, so rail carried roughly **27–32%** (computed). Shuttle buses to Manhattan, a Nutley park-and-ride and 4,700 spaces at American Dream were added ([Wikipedia: Meadowlands station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowlands_station)). This is the lowest car share MetLife has seen, under unusual rules; don't use it as the normal value.
- **Concerts:** NJ Transit said it moved 80,000 people over the 2023 Taylor Swift weekend ([Progressive Railroading](https://www.progressiverailroading.com/passenger_rail/news/Taylor-Swift-fans-boost-ridership-on-US-transit-agencies--69529)). It is unclear whether that counts people or one-way trips, so I can't turn it into a share. A USDOT summary reports traffic delay fell 27% on average across those three Swift nights compared with normal, unlike most Eras Tour stadiums ([USDOT ITS](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2025-b01970)).
- **Rule that matters:** Meadowlands rail runs only for events expected to draw more than 50,000 ([MetLife Stadium](https://www.metlifestadium.com/plan-your-visit/public-transportation)), so smaller stadium events and the racetrack have no train.
- **Event count changed a lot:** 63 events and over 3.5 million guests in 2025, a record ([Pollstar/VenuesNow](https://news.pollstar.com/?p=1226392)), against 25 non-NFL events and 1.3 million in 2023 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250812124644/en/MetLife-Stadium-Further-Enhances-the-Fan-Experience-with-Stadium-Upgrades-Ahead-of-the-NFL-Season)). Concerts now outnumber NFL dates, so a single value blended by attendance is about **80%** (computed, self-audit). Kylie's earlier call was one value; 85% stays defensible for NFL Sundays, 80% for the year.

|---|---|---|---|---|

The "range across checks" runs MSG at 28% and 47%, MetLife at a blended 80% and at a typical (non-record) year, and adds the two amphitheaters. The seat-weighted result lines up with the Oct 6 answer's ~60%.

Two other ways to read the same inputs:

- **By events:** 911 of 1,244 big events (73%) happen at venues where most fans arrive by transit; 80 at Yankee Stadium (half and half); 253 at venues where most drive.
- **By people:** 9.9 M of 21.4 M annual attendances (46%) are at transit-majority venues, 3.4 M (16%) at Yankee Stadium, and 8.1 M (38%) at car-majority venues.

### Which weighting a fan would recognise

"Most fans drive" and "most fans take transit" are claims about people, so **attendance-weighting is the honest test of whether the sentence is true.** It lands at 48–56% by car: neither sentence passes.

**Event-weighting describes the experience** a New Yorker recognises: most of the nights anyone goes out are at MSG, Barclays, Prudential or Radio City, where most of the crowd came by train. But it can't carry "most fans", because the very largest crowds (MetLife, Citi Field) lean the other way.

**Seat-weighting describes capacity, not trips.** It counts MetLife's 82,500 seats as much as if they were used every night, which is why it is the most car-heavy of the three and the least useful for the sentence.

So the sentence has to say both halves, and the event half is the one New Yorkers will check against their own memory.

## 3. The type, spillover, and the sentence

### Hub or transit?

On the section 2 numbers, **transit** fits better. The type's fallback for a future room with no figure would be 40% under transit and 85% under hub. Against the attendance-weighted 48–56%, 40% is 8–16 points off and 85% is 29–37 points off; against the event-weighted 39–47%, 40% is nearly exact.

But today the fallback is unused (all 33 rooms carry their own figure) and the sentence will be a one-off either way, so **the only number a type change actually moves is spillover**, and spillover is where the type is the wrong tool.

### Spillover in New York's clusters

| Cluster | Venues within ~4 miles | How a big crowd spills | Does 10% fit? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Penn District | MSG and its theater, Radio City (~1 mi), Javits | Mostly onto trains and sidewalks; MSG's own data showed no significant event-day change in traffic volume (CPC 2023) | Yes, or lower |
| Queens | Citi Field, US Open grounds; NYCFC's stadium from 2027; casino ~2030 | Same lots, same Mets–Willets Point station, same parkway ramps; the two already compete for parking on overlap dates | No; 15% or more when both are on |
| Meadowlands | MetLife, Meadowlands Racetrack, American Dream | Same Route 3 / Route 120 / Turnpike exits; 80–85% by car; American Dream's spaces were used for World Cup parking | No; 15% or more |
| Long Island | UBS Arena, Belmont Park (same campus) | Same Cross Island Parkway exits and lots | No; 15% or more. Nassau Coliseum and Hofstra are probably outside 4 miles of Elmont (check) |

Spillover depends on **the source venue**, not the city. A simple rule that uses data Fan/Friction already stores: scale the hub weight by the source venue's car share, **spill = 15% × (car share ÷ 85%)**. That gives MSG about 6.5%, Barclays 4.4%, Yankee 8.8%, Citi 10.6%, MetLife 15.0% and UBS 15.9% (computed). Shared-lot pairs (Citi/USTA, MetLife/racetrack, UBS/Belmont) could take a floor of 15% regardless. This is a design suggestion, not a measured value.

**Recommendation:** switch New York to **transit** if per-venue spillover is built at the same time. If it isn't, **keep hub**: a city-wide 10% would understate the three car-heavy clusters, where spillover really is a road problem, to fix a Penn District overstatement that matters less.

### Sentence options (≤70 characters)

| Option | Characters | Evidence that makes it true | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"New York: most events are a train ride; stadium crowds often drive."** (recommended) | 67 | 73% of big events are at transit-majority venues (section 2). MetLife 80–85%, Citi Field 60% and Yankee Stadium 50% by car | Event counts gathered for 12 of 33 rooms only; if the other 21 (Nassau Coliseum, minor-league parks, colleges) push event-weighting past ~47%, use option 2 |
| "New York: about half of fans take transit to events; half drive." | 64 | Attendance-weighted 48–56% by car | True as a count, but flat; it hides that the answer depends on the venue |
| "New York: most ride transit to city arenas; most drive to MetLife." | 66 | MSG 37%, Barclays 25% by car; MetLife 85% | Says nothing about the ballparks or Long Island |

Tested against the Oct 14 answer's concern: none of these tells New Yorkers that most fans drive.

## Self-audit

### Claims on thin evidence

- **MSG 37%.** The figures are MSG's own, quoted by the CPC without survey dates, sample sizes or method. MSG was arguing it is compatible with Penn Station, which could tilt it toward showing high transit use, so the true car share could be at the top of the range rather than the bottom. Counting taxis as "by car" is a definition choice for Kylie.
- **Radio City 15%, Forest Hills, Theater at MSG:** no data at all.
- **Yankee 50%, Citi 60%, Barclays 25%, Prudential 45%:** all 11–19 years old; nothing newer exists, and nothing found would clearly move them.
- **MetLife World Cup share:** computed from tickets sold, not riders counted; buses and park-and-ride not included.
- **Swift weekend 80,000:** unclear whether people or trips; not used.
- **US Open "more than 60%":** a promotional statement, not a survey.
- **Event-weighted sentence (option 1):** event counts cover the ten brief venues plus two amphitheaters, not all 33 rooms.

### Figures from memory, not from a source in this session

- Louis Armstrong Stadium (~14,000) and Grandstand (~8,000) seats; Forest Hills capacity (~13,000); Jones Beach (~15,000) and PNC (~17,500) capacities and their ~40 shows and ~0.45 M attendance each; Seton Hall home-game count; distances from Elmont to Nassau Coliseum and Hofstra; all of section 4.
- Seats used for MSG (19,500), Barclays (18,000) and Prudential (17,500) are my midpoints between basketball/hockey and concert capacities.

### Numbers I computed

- **MSG 37%:** (23 + 38) ÷ 2 = 30.5 private; (5 + 9) ÷ 2 = 7 taxi; 30.5 + 7 = 37.5 → 37. Range 23 + 5 = 28 to 38 + 9 = 47.
- **MSG attendance 4.0 M:** 235 events × 17,000 (assumed average) = 3.995 M.
- **Barclays per year:** ~17 M ÷ ~11.3 years ≈ 1.5 M; 2,000 ÷ 11.3 ≈ 177 events → ~180.
