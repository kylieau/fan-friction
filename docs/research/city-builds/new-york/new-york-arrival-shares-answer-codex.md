# New York event arrivals: venue evidence, weighting, and an honest city sentence

Research date: October 14, 2026. Prepared by Codex.

## Decision in plain words

**Replace the city sentence now. Keep `hub` provisionally; this research does not establish the metro-wide attendance majority needed to justify changing it to `transit`.** Keeping the code is a decision to avoid an unsupported spillover change, not a finding that most fans drive.

Use: **“New York: fans ride trains or drive, depending on the venue.”** It is 60 characters, including spaces and punctuation, without the quotation marks.

There are useful updates: Citi Field's 2024 operational figures imply **62.4% arriving in cars, including rideshare**; UBS reports **66% driving in 2024–25**, then **63% for the incomplete 2025–26 period**; Forest Hills' operator reports a survey finding **25% car/rideshare and 75% train**. MSG supplied a **51–61% public-transit range in 2023**, without a complete car split. Sources and qualifications follow.

The requested three **whole-metro** percentages cannot all be verified from the public records located. A ten-destination capacity model gives **54.7% car**. Event- and attendance-weighted metro totals remain **unestablished**. A deliberately limited, comparable four-destination example gives **38.2% by capacity, 23.4% by published event activity, and 32.7% by attendance**. That example excludes the driving suburbs and must not be advertised as New York's split.

This distinction is the main reconciliation of the two previous answers: capacity and frequency really can pull in different directions, but neither the supplied 60% capacity result nor an intuitive frequency argument proves a current metro-wide majority.

## Scope, definitions, and evidence labels

The two earlier research files were not available in the supplied attachment or found by filename in the accessible Documents/Codex workspace. Their conclusions and stored values are taken from the brief, not independently reread. The complete 33-room list, exact room values, prior 13-venue capacity inputs, and app event ledger were not supplied. This report does not modify app data.

For road gridlock, define **car arrival** as a person arriving at the venue area in a private car, taxi, car service, limousine, or rideshare vehicle. Count a person who drives to a distant station and finishes by train as a rail arrival for the venue's local road load. Do not count someone walking from a parking garage as a walk-only arrival. Treat buses separately when possible: their passengers are not equivalent to the same number of car passengers.

The labels below mean:

- **Measured:** a described survey or operational count. A measured component is not necessarily a measured complete split.
- **Reported:** an organization or publication supplies an outcome, but the underlying count, sampling, or conversion is not fully disclosed.
- **Announced:** a plan, forecast, service offer, or target; not an observed outcome.
- **Estimated:** a model value or arithmetic inference. Estimated results below use the label even when their inputs include measurements.

“Newest found” means newest located and inspected in this search, not a guarantee that no unpublished or unindexed study exists. Undated pages are identified as such; crawl dates are not survey dates. All stored percentages are **reported by the user, October 2026**; their original evidence dates are listed separately where known.

## 2. Which weighting answers the sentence?

### The right denominator

**Attendance weighting** answers “how most fans arrive” if “fans” means attendance visits: sum car-arriving admissions, divide by all admissions. It still does not describe distinct people, or only New York residents. Most public sources do not separate locals from visitors.

**Event weighting** answers “what kind of arrival pattern is typical across event occurrences.” It gives a barely qualifying show and a stadium crowd equal weight. It can help describe the experience of browsing dates, but it does not count most people.

**Capacity weighting** answers a hypothetical simultaneous full-house question. It is useful for capacity exposure; it is the weakest basis for a sentence about annual fan behavior.

For each venue/event class, with car share `p`, capacity `C`, qualifying event count `E`, and annual attendance `A`:

- Seat-weighted car share = `sum(C × p) / sum(C)`.
- Event-weighted car share = `sum(E × p) / sum(E)`.
- Attendance-weighted car share = `sum(A × p) / sum(A)`.

Where classes differ, perform the latter two sums by class. A majority not arriving by car is also not automatically a majority taking transit or walking: bicycles, charter buses and unclassified modes remain.
