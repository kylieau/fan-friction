# Portland metro: event-travel evidence and city-type assessment for Fan/Friction Gridlock

**Research date:** Monday, October 12, 2026. All sources were accessed on this date. Public sources only.
**Scope:** Portland–Vancouver–Hillsboro event metro (Oregon and Washington). Outer counties, Salem, Eugene and distant amphitheaters are treated separately.
**Settings being assessed (from the brief, not re-derived):** `sprawl` 0.85, `hub` 0.85, `transit` 0.40 fallback car share; venue-specific shares override.

---

## 0. How to read this answer

**Definitions used throughout**

| Term | Meaning in this answer |
|---|---|
| Approach-area car share | Share of **people** arriving by private car parked near the venue, carpool, ridehail/taxi or drop-off. These cars use the venue's own approach streets. |
| Car-involved share | Approach-area car share **plus** people who drove to a park-and-ride or remote lot and finished by transit or shuttle. This follows the brief: a drive to remote parking plus a shuttle is car-involved. |
| Recommended value | The car-involved share, because the brief requires remote-parking/park-and-ride trips to count as car-involved. The approach-area value is shown alongside because park-and-ride cars load distant roads (e.g., US-26 near Sunset Transit Center), not the venue's streets. |
| Measured / Projected / Estimated | Measured = observed survey of event attendees. Projected = a plan target or forecast. Estimated = my transparent inference from comparators; always labelled with range and confidence. |

**People vs. vehicles.** All shares are shares of attendees (people), not vehicles. Example: at Providence Park in 2019 the survey found an average of 2.39 fans per parked car ([2019 CTMP Season Report, p. 8–9](https://efiles.portlandoregon.gov/Record/13533027/File/Document)), so a 47% drive-and-park share is far fewer vehicles than 47% of seats.

**Companion venue research.** The companion venue answer was **not available** to me. I enumerated venues independently (Section 2) and list coverage gaps there. Row keys (V01, E01, …) are my own; please map them to the companion answer's keys when it is available.

## 4. Ridership inference: what can and cannot be derived

**Timbers Ticket2Ride (2024).** TriMet's slides show game-day vs. non-game-day totals of about 600,000 vs. 215,000 passenger rides (+179%) and 375,000 vs. 175,000 Hop taps (+114%) ([TriMet slides](https://hosted-files.sched.co/oats2026/7d/Wed%201430%20Ballroom%20-%20OATS_TriMet%20Ticket2Ride%20Zoolights%20Pilot_All%20slides.pdf)). The ratios match the stated percentages, but the slides do not state the number of days, the stations included, or whether non-attendee trips are excluded.

Illustrative sensitivity, **not adopted**:

| Step | Assumption | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline subtraction | 600,000 − 215,000 | 385,000 incremental rides |
| Days covered | 17 home league matches (assumed) / 20 matches (assumed) | 22,600 / 19,250 incremental rides per match |
| Return trips | Each attendee rides twice | ≈11,300 / ≈9,600 riders per match |
| Denominator | 2024 Timbers average 22,485 ([MLS table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Soccer_attendance)) | ≈50% / ≈43% transit |
| Biases | Transfers count as extra rides (overstates); other downtown trips on game days (overstates); unknown station scope (either way) | Not defensible as a mode share |

Conclusion: transit use at Providence Park probably rose above the 2019 measured 33%, but the size of the rise is unknown. The Timbers CEO's claim that nearly 80% of fans used their ticket to ride during the season counts fans who rode at least once, not riders per match (same slides), so it is not a mode share either. Survey shares from 2024–2025 shown in the slides (awareness, frequency) are not mode splits.

**Post Malone concert (June 28, 2025).** 855 taps vs. 558 and 432 on two comparison Saturdays gives +297 to +423 taps (same slides). There is no attendance denominator, taps exclude riders using event tickets as fare, and taps count both directions. No mode share is derived.

**Parking supply.** Space counts (Rose Quarter, Hillsboro, Providence Park garages) are reported for context only and are never converted into shares.

### 5c. Recommendation

**Recommended existing city type: `hub` (in between).**

The measured downtown stadium is car-involved at about 0.69 (approach-area about 0.54), estimated downtown arena rows fall around 0.72–0.78, and suburban or exurban venues sit at 0.90 or higher. That spread matches an in-between region: a transit-supported central cluster surrounded by car-dependent venues. Nothing measured or estimated comes near the `transit` default of 0.40.

**Strongest contrary evidence**

| Direction | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Toward `transit` | Providence Park's approach-area car share (0.54) is closer to the middle of 0.40 and 0.85 than to 0.85, and Ticket2Ride probably lowered it further after 2019 (Section 4). |

## 8. End summary

### 1. Recommended existing city type and rationale; strongest contrary evidence

**`hub`.** Central Portland shows a measured 0.69 car-involved share at Providence Park and estimated 0.72–0.78 at the Rose Quarter, while outer venues are 0.90+. Strongest contrary evidence: Providence Park's approach-area share (0.54) and probable post-2024 transit gains pull toward `transit` for that venue; the car-dominant 18,000-seat Ridgefield amphitheater and 2026–2028 TriMet cuts pull toward `sprawl`.
