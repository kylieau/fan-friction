Research how an event-traffic app should handle regional events: big events outside a metro's core that still affect its traffic or draw its residents. Think state and county fairs, multi-day music festivals, motorsports, air shows, large rodeos, and college games in nearby towns. The app maps events with likely crowds over 5,000 by metro (e.g. LA, Bay Area, Chicago) and scores each day's traffic friction.

Answer:

1. Inclusion rule: what distance or drive-time radius from a metro's core makes sense (e.g. 60 miles, a 90-minute drive, the metro's statistical area)? Compare the options and recommend one.
2. Scoring: should a regional event count toward the metro's daily traffic score, show on the map only, or appear in a separate "Further out" list? When does an out-of-town event actually affect in-metro traffic (e.g. shared freeways, airports, departure surges)?
3. Sources: where to reliably find these events and their crowd sizes (official sites, state fair associations, Pollstar, festival lineups, sanctioning bodies like NASCAR, PRCA). Rank them by reliability and coverage.
4. Crowd sizing: how to estimate daily attendance for multi-day events, with past attendance as the default.
5. Examples: for LA, the Bay Area and Chicago, list 5–10 real recurring regional events each, with location, typical dates, drive time from the core and typical daily crowd, citing a source for every number.

Flag anything uncertain instead of guessing. End with a recommended rule set in 6 bullets or fewer.
