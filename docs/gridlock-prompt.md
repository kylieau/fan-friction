I'm building a personal app called Fan/Friction. For any date in a city, it shows how much competition from other big live events (sports and concerts) shapes who goes to what, and how hard the night is for getting around. It starts in Los Angeles, but every rule has to work in any city in the US or abroad. I'd like a critical second opinion on one open piece: **how to score "Gridlock."** Push back where you disagree and propose something better. Say where a suggestion only fits LA or only fits the US.

**What's already decided (don't reopen unless you think it's broken).**
- Only facts known before an event count. Results and actual attendance never feed a score (earlier results, like pre-game standings, are fine). Size means **venue capacity**, never expected attendance.
- Each date gets two 1–10 sub-scores. **Crowd fight** is fans choosing between events (already designed: roughly the competitor's share of the two venues' combined seats, times an overlap weight and a time-overlap factor). **Gridlock** is everyone converging on shared roads, rail, parking and (for out-of-town crowds) airports and hotel areas, whether or not they like the same things. It is computed once per date from a table of bottlenecks, so no crowd is counted twice.
- The date rating is the louder of the two, plus a small bump when the other is also high (placeholder: 0.5 × how far the quieter one is above 3, capped at 10). Both sub-scores show to users only at Moderate or higher. The date rating measures how hard the night was for fans, including getting around.
- **v1 uses only free, public data.** No billed traffic data (Google Maps historical traffic is charged per request, Waze is partner-only, Placer.ai and StreetLight are paid). Traffic in the app is an estimate of our own, never live.
- Each city picks one of three starting types: drive-centric sprawl (LA, Dallas, Houston), transit-dominant (New York, Chicago, London), destination hub (Las Vegas, Orlando), plus a few venue exceptions.
- Events of about 5,000–8,000 seats (a mid-size theater) enter the model only when they share a campus or corridor with a headline sports or stadium event that night. Invite-only events (awards shows, conventions) count in Gridlock by capacity but carry no Crowd fight. Family shows count only at 5,000+ seats and only when they share a bottleneck with another event inside its arrival or exit window.
- Events that are routes or closures (marathons, parades, street festivals) have no venue. They count as public-capacity limits that raise Gridlock on the corridors they close.

**The current Gridlock draft (the thing to improve).**
- Bottlenecks are listed per city: freeway corridors and interchanges, rail lines and interchange stations, parking pools, and (for national or regional draws) the airport, interstate entries and hotel districts.
- `load(b) = sum over events using b of: venue capacity × mode share × window overlap × multiplier`. Mode share comes from the city type, a venue exception, or US census commute data. Arrival window is 2 hours before the start, exit window is 1 hour after the expected end. Multiplier is 1.0 local, 1.15 regional draw, 1.3 national draw (placeholders).
- Each bottleneck gets a 1–10 score from its load against a reference capacity (parking spaces, line capacity, a corridor baseline). Date Gridlock = the worst bottleneck score plus 0.5 for each other "stressed" bottleneck (5 or more), capped at 10 (placeholders).
- A pre-announced strike, line closure, engineering works or police shortage sets the affected bottleneck to 10.
- **The cost problem:** the bottleneck list and reference capacities are manual local work for every city, and I'm one person.

**Questions.**

What is the minimum data per city that still gives sensible answers? Propose a concrete, lighter design and say what it gives up compared with the full bottleneck table.
2. **How should load map to a 1–10 score?** Give a concrete formula or lookup table. The report I'm replacing divided by a "reference capacity" without saying how to find one in a new city. What free, public sources could set a reference (parking counts, rail line capacity, road capacity, transit ridership), and what do you use when none exists?
3. **A scale that works across city types.** Should Gridlock be absolute (so a 90,000-seat stadium always loads more than an arena), relative to the city's infrastructure, or something else? Show what each does to Kansas City, a one-team city with large stadiums and light traffic, and to London, where rail absorbs very large crowds.
4. **A transit adjustment.** How should rail and bus share change the score, per venue? Propose a simple modifier and the free data behind it (for the US, census commute data; elsewhere?). How should it treat a planned strike or engineering works?
5. **Invite-only and mid-size events.** How should an invite-only event of about 7,000 (an awards show) or a theater next to an arena count? Same as ticketed, or with a smaller weight?
