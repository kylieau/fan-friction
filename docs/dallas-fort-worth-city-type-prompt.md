## The region
One line each: is Dallas–Fort Worth, for a night out at these venues, a **driving region** (most crowds 80%+ by car, like Los Angeles), a **transit region**, or **in between**, and why.

Then **say where the answer breaks by venue**. My expectation, to confirm or correct: Arlington (AT&T, Globe Life, Choctaw) is near-total car with a rideshare minority; American Airlines Center takes a real DART and TRE share at Victory; Fair Park and the Cotton Bowl take a real Green Line share, especially during the State Fair; Frisco, Allen, Fort Worth and Denton are car. Give a number per venue.

**The Arlington double date:** when the Cowboys and Rangers, or a Cowboys game and a Globe Life concert, fall on the same day, what do the city's and teams' traffic plans say about how long clearing the district takes, and are there published counts? That is the clearest Gridlock case in the region.

## Known leads (verify, don't trust)
- **DART publishes ridership by station and day**; Victory Station on Mavericks and Stars nights and Fair Park station during the State Fair and on Red River Showdown day, against announced crowds, come close to an observed share. The TRE's Victory counts add the Fort Worth side.
- **Arlington's Via rideshare** and its autonomous shuttle pilots publish ridership; the city has studied stadium-district traffic repeatedly (the 2004 Cowboys stadium election materials, the 2016 Globe Life Field agreements, the entertainment district master plan).
- **AT&T Stadium's** event transportation plans for the Super Bowl (2011), the College Football Playoff title game and the 2026 World Cup carry mode-split targets and observed results.
- **The State Fair of Texas** reports daily attendance and its DART ridership deal every year.
- **Toyota Stadium's** 2024–2026 renovation approvals (Frisco) carry a traffic study.
- **Dickies Arena's** 2019 opening and the Stock Show's traffic plans (Fort Worth).
- **NCTCOG** (the regional council) publishes special-event traffic studies.
