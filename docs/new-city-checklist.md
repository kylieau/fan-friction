# New-city checklist

Kylie, Oct 6, 2026: Los Angeles is the only city built by hand. Every later city rides this page and the scripts. If a step can't be put here, the feature isn't done. A second city is for testing that the formula holds outside LA (step 4 of `docs/big-picture-plan-oct6.md`), not for coverage.

## 1. Configure (a few lines)
- `src/config/metros.ts`: id, name, time zone, map center and zoom.
- `src/data/formula/gridlock.ts` `CITY_TYPE`: sprawl (everyone drives), hub, or transit. This sets the spill between zones and the default car share for a venue without its own. Research it, don't assume it (Kylie, Oct 6): run `docs/seattle-city-type-prompt.md` with the city swapped in, and give each venue its `carShare` from the answer.
- `src/data/formula/overlap.ts`: the city's **broad teams** (everyone's team, by year), if any.
- `src/data/formula/weather.ts` `MONTHLY_NORMAL_F`: the heat baseline by month (empty today; the 85°F floor applies until filled). Needs a source that works for every city.
- `src/data/scheduleArchive.ts` / `scripts/weather-fetch.mjs` / `scripts/results-fetch.mjs` / `scripts/catalog-write.mjs` (`ARCHIVE_METRO_ID`, `RESULTS_METRO_ID`): the metro ids each job covers (LA only today). The shared catalog tables are keyed by metro, so a second city needs no new tables.

## 2. Teams (one line each)
- `src/data/teams.ts`: a record per team and college program (id, names, league, sport, metro).
- `src/data/sources/mlbSource.ts` `MLB_TEAMS` and `MLB_VENUES`: MLB id and ballpark id.
- `src/data/sources/espnSource.ts` `ESPN_TEAMS` and `VENUE_BY_NAME`: ESPN path and id per team; the venue names ESPN uses. Ids are in `docs/data-sources.md`'s method: the team page on espn.com carries the id in its URL.
- `scripts/attendance-collect.mjs`: the same ids with the seasons to pull.

## 3. Venues (the hand part, about an afternoon)
- Run the venue research prompt for the city (`docs/la-venue-table-prompt.md`, change the city and boundary). Fold the answer into `src/data/venues.ts`: names with rename dates, coordinates, capacity by setup and year, roof. Label every figure official, reported or estimated. Geocode coordinates against OpenStreetMap.
- Save the answer under `docs/` and point `docs/data-sources.md` at it. If it arrived as a PDF, save the PDF too and transcribe it with its links (`node scripts/pdf-text.mjs file.pdf`); a paste in the chat is not a saved source.

## 4. Run the scripts
1. `npm run attendance-collect` then `npm run attendance-calibrate`: past crowds and expected draws per team.
2. `node scripts/venue-access.mjs`: relief and streets per venue; the hard-access flag follows from the rule.
3. `npm run archive-schedule` once by hand to confirm the feeds, weather and results all answer for the new metro. The nightly job also writes each team's full schedule (`team_schedules`); a team is covered there as soon as it is in `ESPN_TEAMS` or `MLB_TEAMS`.
4. `node scripts/retune-analysis.mjs` only when the city has a season of archived nights.

## 5. Check on screen
San Diego (Oct 6, 2026) was the first run of this list, about three hours end to end: steps 1, 2 and 4 in an hour (the Padres, the city facts, the jobs' city list, the attendance pull); step 3 from Kylie's one research run (`docs/archive/research/san-diego-venue-table-answer.md`, 13 venues folded in, including Frontwave Arena, which the brief had missed); then SDSU, San Diego FC and the Wave joined the feed and the attendance pull.

Seattle (Oct 6, 2026) was the second run, about ninety minutes once the research answer was in: eight teams and the city facts in one commit, 21 venues folded in (`docs/archive/research/seattle-venue-table-answer.md`), the attendance pull and calibration (12 minutes), the hard-access measure (about 25 minutes, Open-Elevation's pace), one hand run of `archive-schedule`, then the nightly job by hand so the catalog had the city the same day. Two things learned: the venue table's `setup` has no festival kind (store festival caps as `concert` with a note), and ESPN's short name for a soccer club can be the city ("Seattle"), so game titles now take our own short name for a home team we know.

New York (Oct 7, 2026) was the third run, about two and a half hours once both research answers were in, committed and pushed after every step: steps 1–2 in forty minutes (18 teams; **three of the ids written down in advance were wrong** — nhl/1 is the Bruins, nwsl/15362 is Portland, cfb/2523 is Robert Morris — so every id was checked against the feed first, and ESPN's venue names were read from each team's own schedule rather than guessed); step 3 in an hour (33 venues from `docs/archive/research/new-york-venue-table-answer.md`, a car share on each from `docs/archive/research/new-york-city-type-answer.md`, every coordinate checked against OpenStreetMap by name, five moved); step 4 in forty minutes (attendance pull and calibration, one hand archive run, the hard-access measure at Open-Elevation's pace). Four things learned: **research the city type before touching `CITY_TYPE`** — the code had New York as transit from before any research, and the measured answer is hub; a plain team id may already belong to an away team (`giants`, `jets`, `rangers` were San Francisco, Winnipeg and Texas), so prefix it; the research's "Table B" grounds with no fixed capacity (the US Open grounds, Javits, festival parks) are not venue rows — events there are placed as points and sized by their own crowd; and the feeds serve today onward, so a tester's nights from *before* the city was covered need reconstructed reads from saved research, a separate step.

Atlanta (Oct 7, 2026) was the fourth run, about ninety minutes to a saved listing once both research answers were in, committed and pushed after every step. Steps 1–2 in half an hour (14 teams; every id checked against the feed first, all correct this time; the Braves already existed as a visitor record and became the home record, same id); step 3 in forty minutes (20 venues, each with a car share; 16 coordinates confirmed by OpenStreetMap, 3 rooms it does not know taken from the research and marked approximate); step 4: the attendance pull (two minutes), one triggered nightly run (three minutes; Atlanta's first listing had 32 events, 27 with an estimate), and the hard-access measure after an Open-Elevation rate limit (wait five minutes and retry). Five things learned: **a research answer may correct the brief itself** (six venue names had changed since the prompt was written: EchoPark Speedway, Gwinnett Field, Synovus Bank Amphitheater, Lakewood's dropped sponsor), so read the answer's own "out of date" notes before writing rows; **ESPN names some buildings by an interior brand** (the KSU Convocation Center is "VyStar Arena", Fifth Third Stadium is "Walens Family Field at Fifth Third Stadium"), so take the names from each team's own schedule as before; **neutral-site games are hand-listed** (`listings2026.ts`) because neither side is a home team in the feeds; **festival parks and convention halls are not venue rows** (Kylie, Oct 7), as New York's Table B grounds were not; and a city's Explore screen says "No events" until the nightly job has written its catalog, so trigger the job right after step 3 and check the screen after it lands.

The Bay Area (Oct 7, 2026) was the fifth run, about seventy minutes to a saved listing once both research answers were in, committed and pushed after every step. Steps 1–2 in twenty minutes (17 teams, including the Oakland Roots for their last Coliseum match; every id checked against the feed first, and one guess was wrong: Bay FC is nwsl/22187, not 22530; the Giants already existed as a visitor record and became the home record, same id, as the Braves had); step 3 in twenty-five minutes (20 venues, each with a car share from the city-type answer; 18 coordinates confirmed by OpenStreetMap within about 100 m, Frost and the SJSU event center taken from the research and marked approximate); step 4: the attendance pull (one team at a time: `node scripts/attendance-collect.mjs <teamId>` takes a team id, not a city), calibration, one hand archive run, the triggered nightly run (27 events, 13 of them Ticketmaster), and the hard-access measure (about fifteen minutes, no rate limit this time). Four things learned: **the hard-access rule and the research disagree in both directions** (the rule flags Cow Palace, the Concord pavilion and Haas; the research calls Shoreline single-approach and Memorial Stadium and the Greek constrained, none of which the rule flags), so a city's flags are recorded as the rule's and the research's judgment stays in the venue notes; **a team's short name must match ESPN's display name or its games take ESPN's city name** ("Oakland vs. San Antonio" until the Roots' record said "Oakland Roots"); **a team can announce more than its building's listed size** (the 49ers announce about 71,500 at Levi's, listed at 68,500), which the estimate shows honestly beside Seats; and a metro with three centers 30–45 miles apart (San Francisco, Oakland, San Jose) reads every night as one crowd, which is the case for the distance discount (`docs/distance-discount-proposal.md`).

- Explore opens on the city with its venues on the map; the date page reads; an event page shows Outcome, Starters, TV and Length after a game.
- The city appears in Explore's city switcher and offers "Set as home" (both follow from having events; cities with none are not listed, Kylie, Oct 6).
- The Add form lists the city's venues under Where.

## Not per city (already universal)
Feeds, results, game length, the formula, weather, the map, accounts, the Add form's sport list, the hard-access rule.
